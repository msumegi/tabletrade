Lesson 4 of 5

Raise a concern and complain if you need to

If you have concerns about how a business has handled your personal information, the OPC says your first step should generally be to raise the concern directly with the organization. In many cases you may be able to resolve the issue quickly.

Contact the privacy officer at the business. Businesses should take your concerns seriously and work with you to resolve them. Always try this step before you report a concern to the OPC or another privacy office. If you skip it, the OPC may not be able to accept your complaint.

If you have spoken with the organization and you are not satisfied, you have the right to file a complaint with the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

You do not need to hire a consultant or lawyer to help you file a complaint. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), the OPC said that if you have questions about your privacy rights or the complaints process, you can contact its Information Officers by calling 1-800-282-1376. Review the live “Guide to the PIPEDA complaint process” on the OPC website before you lodge a complaint.

A complaint about a business is not the same as a concern about a government office. Federal government institutions have Access to Information and Privacy (ATIP) co-ordinators. A concern about a provincial, territorial, or municipal government does not fall under the OPC’s mandate. Those governments have their own privacy officers. The OPC’s complaint tool can help you find the right office.

This course cannot decide your case. It is general information only.
