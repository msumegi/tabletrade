What PIPEDA protects

When you do business with a company, you do more than pay for a product or service. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) says you may also leave a trail of personal information — for example your name, address, credit card number, and spending habits.

Sharing that information does not mean giving up control over it. Privacy laws in Canada give you a meaningful say in how your personal data is collected, used, and disclosed.

Canada’s federal private-sector privacy law is the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA). It sets the ground rules for handling personal information in the course of commercial activities. It applies to small and big businesses, whether they operate from a building or only online.

PIPEDA applies to private enterprises across Canada, except in provinces that have adopted substantially similar privacy legislation, namely Québec, British Columbia, and Alberta. Even in those provinces, PIPEDA still applies to federally regulated organizations such as radio and television stations, airports and airlines, railways, and telecommunication companies. It also applies to personal data that flows across provincial or national borders in commercial transactions covered by the Act.

PIPEDA protects information about an identifiable individual. Official examples include your name, e-mail address, age, medical records, income, banking information, credit or debit card data, and your Social Insurance Number or other identification numbers.

This course is not legal advice. It does not teach scam emails, passwords, or how to use an AI chatbot. It explains your privacy rights when a business holds information about you.
