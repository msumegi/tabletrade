Ask to see your personal information

PIPEDA gives people a right to access personal information businesses subject to the law hold about them. The OPC says you need to put your request in writing.

Send the request to the address the organization has designated for access requests or privacy issues, if there is one. That address is often in a privacy policy in the website footer. Ask for the privacy officer or the person responsible for privacy compliance.

Be clear and specific. Describe the information you want. A narrower request — for example, a topic or a date range — can lead to a quicker response. Include details so they can identify your records, such as your name, address, and an account number if you have one.

It should cost little or nothing. An organization may charge only a minimal fee if it has told you the approximate cost up front and you agree to proceed.

The organization is supposed to give you access within 30 calendar days, or tell you within 30 days that it does not have the information. In some cases it may extend the time by up to 30 more days — for example if the deadline would unreasonably interfere with its activities, or it needs time for consultations. It must advise you of the delay within the first 30 days and explain why.

If you find a factual error or omission, you may request a correction. You typically need to provide some evidence. If you cannot agree on a change, you have the right to have your concerns recorded.

Ordinarily you may only request information about yourself. A company can deny access in some circumstances set out in law. If you feel it is withholding more than it should, try to resolve that with the organization first. You may then complain to the OPC.
