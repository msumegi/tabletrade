Alberta’s PIPA, when the provincial law applies

Alberta has its own private-sector privacy law: the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA). Alberta says PIPA applies to provincially regulated private-sector organizations, businesses, and, in some instances, non-profits.

The federal law, PIPEDA, still applies to federally regulated businesses such as banks, airlines, and telecommunication companies. The Alberta page notes that the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada reviews privacy complaints for that federal private sector — for example banks (but not Alberta credit unions), airlines, and telecommunications companies. If you are not sure which office to contact, the OPC says this can be complicated and points to its tool for choosing the right organization.

Under PIPA, you have a right to access your personal information held by an organization and to ask the organization to correct it. You must make the request in writing. Many organizations have a privacy officer who receives requests.

Organizations can charge fees for access under PIPA, but not to correct your information and not if you request personal employee information. Fees must be reasonable and follow the regulations.

If the organization refuses to provide your information, does not respond within 45 days unless it appropriately extends the time, or you feel it is not following PIPA, you may complain to Alberta’s Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner (OIPC).

Some information may be withheld, such as information collected for a legal proceeding. Organizations must never provide information that could threaten another person’s life or security, reveal another individual’s personal information, or reveal the identity of someone who gave a confidential opinion about another person without that person’s consent.

If you want information held by a public body, such as a government department or a municipality, that is a different law (the Protection of Privacy Act). This course does not teach that process.
