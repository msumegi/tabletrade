Your rights when a business has your information

PIPEDA requires private-sector organizations to collect, use, or disclose your personal information by fair and lawful means, with your consent, and only for stated, reasonable purposes.

An organization may only collect personal information that is essential to the business transaction. If it asks for more, you are entitled to ask why and to decline it if you are not satisfied with the answer. The OPC says you should still be able to complete the transaction even if you refuse extra information that is not warranted.

Organizations must protect your personal information with appropriate security measures and destroy it when it is no longer needed for the original purposes. You have the right to expect the information they hold about you to be accurate, complete, and up to date. That means you have a right to see it, and to ask for corrections if they got it wrong.

PIPEDA is built on 10 fair information principles. In plain language, organizations should:

  • appoint someone responsible for privacy and make their privacy policies available
  • say why they are collecting your information, at or before the time they collect it
  • inform you clearly and obtain consent
  • collect only what is necessary
  • use, share, and keep it only as needed for that purpose, unless you consent otherwise
  • keep it accurate
  • safeguard it against loss or theft
  • make their privacy practices easy to find
  • give you access to your information
  • offer a simple way to complain

There are exceptions. For example, police who show they need information for an investigation or during an emergency may not have to obtain consent. PIPEDA also does not apply to an employee’s name, title, and business contact details used only to communicate about their work, or to information used solely for personal, journalistic, artistic, or literary purposes.
