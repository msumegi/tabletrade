What counts as data?

Data is information we collect so we can understand something — how many people came to a supper, how much a program cost, or how a number has changed over time. You do not need a special computer program to start thinking about data.

Canada’s Skills for Success include Numeracy: your ability to find, understand, use, and report mathematical information presented through words, numbers, symbols, and graphics. We use this skill to perform calculations, manage budgets, and make sense of numbers we see.

They also include Digital skill: using digital technology and tools to find, manage, apply, create, and share information and content. Organizing information in a clear list is part of that work, whether the list lives on paper, in a notes app, or in a spreadsheet.

Statistics Canada describes a data journey with steps: define, find, and gather data; explore, clean, describe, and understand it; analyze it; and tell the story the data show. A foundation of quality and clear labels supports every step.

This course stays at the beginning of that journey. You will learn to read a table and a simple chart, and to keep a list clean enough that someone else can understand it. You will not learn spreadsheet formulas, sort buttons, or how to insert a chart in Excel. Those are different courses.

When you see a number, pause and ask: What is this about? What time period is it for? Where did it come from?
