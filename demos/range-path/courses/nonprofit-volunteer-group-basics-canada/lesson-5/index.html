Alberta’s “charitable organization” is yet another status

Alberta also uses the phrase charitable organization. That status is easy to confuse with a CRA registered charity, but Alberta says they are not the same.

Registration as a charitable organization in Alberta is done under the Charitable Fund-raising Act. It should not be confused with being a registered charity with the CRA. Alberta says this provincial registration provides a charitable registration number but does not allow the organization to write charitable donation tax receipts. Charitable status for tax purposes is granted only by the CRA.

A charitable organization must be registered under that Alberta Act if:

  • it uses a fundraising business, or
  • it intends to raise more than $25,000 in gross contributions in its financial year from solicitations to individuals in Alberta, or
  • during or after a campaign it finds it has raised more than $25,000 — in that case it must register within 45 days after contributions reach $25,000.

Those dollar figures were on the official page as of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina). Check the live page before you rely on them.

If your group later wants to become a CRA registered charity, go back to Lesson 2: the society’s purposes must meet CRA requirements, and you must apply to the CRA. Advantages Alberta lists for CRA registration include official tax receipts, income-tax exemption, and the ability to receive gifts from other registered charities.

This course cannot file your paperwork, review your purposes, or tell you that your fundraiser is “fine.” It is a map of official differences so volunteer groups do not mix up three different statuses. For a real decision, read the live pages and get legal or tax advice.
