A CRA registered charity is different from a non-profit

The Canada Revenue Agency says registered charities and non-profit organizations (NPOs) both operate without the intention of making a profit, but they are not the same.

A registered charity is a type of qualified donee under the Income Tax Act. It may issue official donation receipts. It is established and operated for exclusively charitable purposes, and it does not pay income tax. It must use all of its resources for charitable activities that advance those purposes.

Charitable purposes fit these categories:

  • relief of poverty
  • advancement of education
  • advancement of religion
  • other purposes that benefit the community

CRA examples include food banks, schools, places of worship, animal shelters, libraries, and volunteer fire departments.

An NPO is an association, club, or society organized and operated exclusively for social welfare, civic improvement, pleasure, recreation, or any other purpose except profit. An NPO is not a qualified donee and cannot issue official donation receipts. CRA examples include some gardening clubs, book clubs, amateur sports groups, and festival organizations.

An NPO is not registered with the CRA as a charity and does not receive a charitable registration number. A registered charity must apply to become one and is issued a number. If you are operating as a charity, CRA says you cannot be considered an NPO.

Both kinds of organization generally cannot use their income to personally benefit their members (with a narrow exception on the CRA page for certain amateur-athletics groups).

Wanting to help the community is not the same as being allowed to issue a tax receipt. Only a CRA registered charity has that authority.
