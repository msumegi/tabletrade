Know what you are applying for

This course is for people who help a community group, council, or small organization look at government funding in Canada. It also helps if you support a small business that wants to search official programs. It does not promise that you will receive money. Finishing the course does not make you a paid grant consultant.

Government support is not only “a grant.” Innovation Canada’s Business Benefits Finder groups many kinds of help. A grant is money you may not have to repay if you follow the program’s rules. Other supports can include loans (money you repay), tax credits, wage subsidies, chances to work with researchers, and expert advice. The right tool depends on your project and on who you are. A registered non-profit is not the same as a for-profit business.

The Government of Alberta says there is work to do before you even start completing a grant application. Innovation Canada asks you to check fit first:

  • Is this project already part of your 12-to-24-month plan?
  • Would you still do the work without the funding?
  • Do you have the people and time to deliver the project and report on it?
  • Are you applying because the program fits, or only because the money exists?

Processing can take weeks or months. If you need to start tomorrow, a slow program may not be the right match. Innovation Canada also notes that government funding is designed to share risk, not to pay every cost. Many business programs expect the applicant to contribute, and asking for 100% of the costs is often a non-starter. That is a general tip for business funding, not a rule for every community grant. Always read the live program page.
