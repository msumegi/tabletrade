Why practise Spanish, and how to say hello

Canada’s two official languages are English and French. Many other languages are also part of daily life. Statistics Canada, using the 2021 Census, reported that Spanish was the non-official language spoken by the most people in Canada (about 1,171,000 speakers). That figure comes from a Statistics Canada page released on January 22, 2025. Numbers can be updated in later censuses — check the live page.

You may hear Spanish at work, in a shop, at a community event, or from a neighbour. A few clear phrases can help you greet someone with respect. This course does not measure your language level and does not replace an English or French class.

Skills for Success communication means receiving, understanding, considering, and sharing information by speaking, listening, and interacting. Practise out loud. Listen for the rhythm. Spanish uses upside-down punctuation at the start of questions and exclamations: ¿ and ¡.

Useful greetings:

  • Hola — Hello
  • Buenos días — Good morning
  • Buenas tardes — Good afternoon
  • Buenas noches — Good evening or good night
  • Adiós — Goodbye
  • Hasta luego — See you later

Say Hola with a clear “o” sound, like “OH-lah.” Smile and speak at a calm pace. Spanish words and greetings can vary from country to country. These are widely understood starting points, not the only “correct” Spanish in the world.
