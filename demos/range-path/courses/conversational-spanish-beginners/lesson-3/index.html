Simple questions and asking for help

A good next step is asking how someone is, and asking for help when you do not understand.

  • ¿Cómo está usted? — How are you? (formal)
  • ¿Cómo estás? — How are you? (informal)
  • Bien, gracias. — Fine, thank you.
  • Más o menos. — So-so.
  • Mal. — Not well.
  • ¿Y usted? — And you?

When you need help with the language itself:

  • No entiendo. — I don’t understand.
  • Más despacio, por favor. — More slowly, please.
  • ¿Puede repetir, por favor? — Can you repeat that, please?
  • ¿Habla inglés? — Do you speak English?
  • Un poco. — A little.

Skills for Success communication includes listening with intention: pay attention, notice gestures, and ask questions to confirm what you heard. If you miss a word, it is better to say No entiendo than to guess and nod.

You can mix a Spanish courtesy word with a plain request: Perdón. No entiendo. Más despacio, por favor.

Practise the question mark at both ends: ¿ at the start and ? at the end. That written mark tells the reader a question is coming.

These phrases will not make you fluent. They give you a polite way to slow the conversation down and keep talking.
