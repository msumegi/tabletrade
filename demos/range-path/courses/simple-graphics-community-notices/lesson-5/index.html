Lesson 5 of 5

Check the notice, then download or share it

Before you print or post, read the notice as if you had never seen it. Can a new person tell what, when, where, and how to ask a question? Are the words spelled correctly? Is the date still right? Is the contrast strong? Did you avoid ALL CAPS and a busy background?

If you work in Word or PowerPoint, ESDC notes that many programs include an accessibility checker. The checker is a help, not a guarantee.

If you used Canva, the official Help page “Download or save designs to your device” says you can save a copy to your computer or phone. On that page, the steps are: go to Share, then select Download, choose a file type, adjust any options you need, and select Download again. If the design has more than one page, you can download all pages, the page you are viewing, or chosen pages.

Canva Help also says that some elements are premium. If your design includes those, a free account may be asked to pay for a licence, upgrade, or download a watermarked draft. To avoid that surprise, stick to free elements and your own photos. This course does not ask you to buy extra content.

A PDF is often a good choice for printing or emailing because the layout stays in place. A PNG or JPG picture file can work for social media. Check the live Help page if the menu looks different from what you see here.

Then ask one other person to read the notice. If they miss the time or the place, fix it before you post.
