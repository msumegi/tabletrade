Lesson 4 of 5

Use pictures to support the words, not replace them

A photo or icon can help people notice your poster. It should not be the only place the facts live.

ESDC says you should use images only to enhance information that is already in the text, not to replace it. If you include an image, add a short description. In a digital file that can be alternative text (alt text) or a caption beside the picture. People who use a screen reader hear that description. People who magnify a page may also find a large photo hard to see if it becomes blurry.

When you write a description:

  • Say what the image shows and why it is there.
  • Keep the words simple and short.
  • You do not need to start with “an image of…” because the software already knows it is an image.

The Digital Accessibility Toolkit says a good test is to imagine describing the picture to someone over the phone. Keep short alt text brief. If a picture is only decoration and adds no meaning, mark it as decorative when the tool allows that.

If your notice will be printed and pinned to a board, the words on the poster still have to carry the facts. A photo of the hall does not replace the street address.

Use only photos you have the right to use — for example, a picture your group took, or an image your tool says you may use. Do not copy a random picture from the internet just because it looks nice. This course is not legal advice about copyright.
