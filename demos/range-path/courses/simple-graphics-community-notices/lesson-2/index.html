Make the text easy to see and read

Once the words are ready, choose a layout that people can actually read from a hallway, a bulletin board, or a phone.

Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) recommends:

  • a font size of 12 points or larger for documents
  • sans-serif fonts (fonts without little hooks on the letters), such as Helvetica, Calibri, Arial, Geneva, or Verdana
  • avoiding serif fonts such as Times New Roman and Garamond for this kind of document
  • avoiding long stretches of italics or words in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS
  • using bold only for a little emphasis
  • aligning long text to the left (centred or fully “justified” text can be harder to read)
  • strong contrast between the text colour and the background — black text on a white background is best
  • not relying only on colour, bold, or an asterisk to mark important information

For presentation slides, ESDC says to use a sans-serif font large enough to read from a distance (at least 18 point) and not to fill a slide with too much information. Aim for a few short bullet points rather than paragraphs. A hallway poster follows the same idea: large, clear text and lots of empty space.

The Digital Accessibility Toolkit adds that text must have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 against the background (as of 10 November 2026, America/Regina). Black on white is always a safe choice. Avoid busy background images behind text. ESDC also says to avoid watermarks or background pictures that weaken contrast.

Do not use colour alone to mean “this is urgent.” Add the word “important” or a short sentence that says the same thing.
