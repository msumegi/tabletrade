Write the words before you decorate

A community notice is a poster, flyer, or digital graphic that tells people about an event, a service, or a change. Pretty decorations do not help if the facts are missing or hard to understand.

Government of Canada accessibility guidance says documents should use simple, clear, and concise language. Keep sentences short. Use common words. Use an active voice when you can (say “We meet on Friday,” not “A meeting will be held”). Avoid jargon unless you must use it, and define a short form the first time it appears.

The Digital Accessibility Toolkit says plain language helps everyone, including people with cognitive disabilities. It suggests short sentences, a reading level around Grade 8, and no unusual words when a simple word will do.

Before you open a design tool, write the facts people need:

  • What is happening?
  • Who is it for?
  • When is it (date and time)?
  • Where is it?
  • What should people bring or do?
  • How can they ask a question (a phone number, email, or office)?

If you cannot answer those questions on paper, the graphic is not ready. This course does not pick colours or logos for your council. It helps you put clear words first, then place them so people can find, understand, and use the information.

You can later put those words into Word, PowerPoint, or an online design tool such as Canva. This course is not a Canva certificate, and you do not have to use that tool.
