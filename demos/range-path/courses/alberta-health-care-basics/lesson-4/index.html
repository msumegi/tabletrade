Your health card and finding a provider

After you register, you receive a personal health card. The health-cards page says the card is proof you are eligible for medically necessary physician services, insured hospital services, and some dental and oral surgical services under AHCIP.

Alberta describes more than one card type: an integrated card (a driver’s licence or ID card with your personal health number on the back), a mobile health card in Alberta Wallet, and a standalone paper card. All of them provide access to insured services.

If you have a standalone or mobile card, always present it with valid photo identification when you visit a doctor, hospital, or other health facility in Alberta, or while visiting another province or territory.

As of November 2026, the page said that effective July 2, 2026, new health cards include an expiry date. Coverage stays valid if the card is not expired and you still live in Alberta and meet eligibility rules. You must revalidate at a registry agent on or before the expiry date. Check the live page for your card type.

To find someone taking patients, Primary Care Alberta and Alberta Find a Provider point you to albertafindaprovider.ca. You can look for a clinic, family doctor, or nurse practitioner taking patients near you. The site also describes walk-in clinics as non-emergency care without an appointment for minor illnesses, injuries, and general health. Having a regular family doctor or team is described there as helpful for ongoing care.

This course does not book your appointment.
