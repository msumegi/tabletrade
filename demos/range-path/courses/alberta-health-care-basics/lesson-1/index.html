Finding the right door: 911, 811, and AHCIP

Alberta’s public health plan is the Alberta Health Care Insurance Plan (AHCIP). The official AHCIP page says it is a publicly funded program that provides eligible Alberta residents with coverage for medically necessary physician and hospital services in Alberta and in other Canadian provinces. Residents must register to receive those services. The page also says AHCIP does not cover everything. Albertans often need extra insurance for expenses such as prescription drugs, dental care, and vision care.

This course does not tell you which clinic to visit for a symptom, and it does not treat you.

Alberta’s “Know Your Options” page says: if you are unsure where to go for care, call Health Link 811 for 24/7 advice on your symptoms and care options. The same page says to call 911 if you or someone you know is having a medical emergency. MyHealth Alberta’s important-numbers page lists 911 for ambulance, fire, and police, and 811 for free nurse advice and general health information, both 24 hours.

Primary Care Alberta’s newcomers page says that for most health concerns, primary care is the first step. That includes family doctors, nurse practitioners, and other providers who help with everyday needs such as checkups, prescriptions, long-term conditions, mental health concerns, and referrals.

If you are in crisis because of suicide, the Know Your Options page also lists 988 for immediate suicide crisis supports. That is separate from 811.
