Follow the instructions on the posting

About 3 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

Canada’s Skills for Success Digital skills include using online forms for things such as purchases, opening accounts, and job applications (learning-steps page, opened 10 November 2026). That is why this lesson exists: applying is often a form, an email, or an account — not only a paper resume.

Alis says employers may ask for a resume and letter (by email, a website, or in person), or for an application form that everyone fills in the same way, or for both. Read what they want. Use the format and delivery method they prefer. Apply to more than one suitable job rather than waiting to hear back from the first; many employers only contact people they plan to interview.

Keep a copy of what you sent and the date, on the master list from Lesson 1. Name the files clearly so the resume and the letter match the same employer.

If you apply by email, *Outlook and Workplace Email Habits* can help with sending and signing out on a shared computer. If you fill a web form, type only what the form asks and what you are willing to share. You can browse many Job Bank listings without an account; you need an account for extra tools. Double-check spelling of the employer’s name before you submit. A form that asks for the same facts as your resume — dates, duties, education — should match the resume you attached, so the two documents do not tell two different stories. If you are using a public computer, finish, save your own copies, and sign out of the email or Job Bank session.

Job Bank accounts, alerts, and your SIN

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

Job Bank’s find-a-job page (opened 10 November 2026) says a Standard account can be started with an email address. With it you can customize searches, get daily Job Alerts, and use resume templates.

A Plus account adds more tools, such as matching you with openings from a profile, invitations from employers, and applying from some postings with a saved resume or one made in the Resume Builder.

Some extra services, including Job Match and Direct Apply, ask you to confirm that you are a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or a temporary resident. To prove eligibility, Job Bank may ask for your date of birth, a parent’s last name at birth, and your valid Social Insurance Number (SIN). Job Bank says the SIN is checked automatically and will not be kept in its records.

If you cannot provide that information or prefer not to, Job Bank says you can still sign up by opting out of those extra services. You can add the information later if you change your mind. This course will not tell you which immigration category you are in. You should not type your SIN into this course, a quiz, or a practice file.

When a posting offers Direct Apply, follow the official steps on Job Bank and read any box that asks you to share your email and documents with that employer. Keep your Job Bank password private, just as you would for email.

A message about a “guaranteed” attendant job asks you to pay a fee and to send a banking password before you can submit a resume. What should you do?

Yes. A fee or a password to guarantee work is a warning sign, not an official Job Bank step. This course never asks you to send banking details.

Do not pay. Official Job Bank and alis pages do not ask you to buy a guaranteed job. Ask a person at your council if a posting feels wrong.

Never send a banking password to apply for work. Stop and tell council staff or use official reporting routes they suggest.

Attachments, forms, and knowing when to stop

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

Before you click Send or Submit, check the To address or the official website, the file names, and whether you attached the resume you tailored for that job. If a form asks for information you do not understand — for example a status question you cannot answer — do not guess. Ask a tutor, Alberta Supports, or settlement staff. Guessing an immigration category in a hurry is how people get into trouble; this course will not walk you through those answers.

You can keep using Job Bank as a guest-style browser for public listings even if you opt out of extra services. Alerts and saved resumes are helpful, not required, for this course’s capstone. The capstone uses our fictional Sampletown posting, not a live apply button.

If a site looks different from a practice screen, trust the live official page and a tutor, not a drawing. Job Bank account rules can change; the facts in this lesson are dated 10 November 2026. Take a breath before you submit. Reread any box that says you are about to share your email or documents with an employer. If you are not sure what a field means, leave it and ask rather than inventing an answer. You can save a draft on paper: the job title, the site you used, the files you sent, and the date. That paper line on your master list is part of applying with care, not extra decoration. It also helps if you later get an interview and the live posting is gone.

When would a Job Alert help you, and when would it just fill your inbox?

Apply online — take-home steps

  • Follow the posting’s method (email, form, or in person) exactly
  • Keep copies and dates on your master list
  • A Standard Job Bank account starts with an email address
  • Some extras ask for a SIN; Job Bank says it will not keep the SIN, and you can opt out
  • Never type a SIN, password, or bank number into this course
  • Stop if someone asks for a fee or a password to guarantee a job
