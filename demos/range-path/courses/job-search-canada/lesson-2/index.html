Build a resume that matches the job

A resume (alis often writes resumé) tells an employer who you are and what you can do. Job Bank says a good resume shows your background, skills, and qualifications, and that you should tailor it to the job by highlighting relevant accomplishments.

Alis suggests starting with a master resume — one long list of your contact information, education and training, work history (most recent job first), skills, accomplishments, volunteer work, awards, and references. Update it often. Then copy the most useful parts into a shorter resume for each job.

Alis describes three common layouts:

  • Chronological — work and education from newest to oldest. Helpful when the job is similar to work you have done.
  • Functional — focuses on skills. May help if you have gaps in paid work.
  • Combination — skills first, then a short work history. Useful if you are changing fields.

Official tips that help:

  • Study the posting and use the same keywords the employer used (alis notes that some software screens resumes for those words).
  • Keep the version you send short — alis says 1 to 2 pages at most.
  • Use a simple, easy-to-read font and point form. Alis says to avoid tables or columns because some screening software cannot read them well.
  • Submit a Word file (.doc or .docx) if the posting allows it; alis says some screening software cannot read PDF files.
  • Be honest. Proofread out loud, use spell check carefully (it may not know cheque from check), and ask a trusted person to read it.

Job Bank has a free Resume Builder after you sign in. It currently describes a traditional template and a template for youth or recent graduates. You fill in the blanks and can save more than one resume.
