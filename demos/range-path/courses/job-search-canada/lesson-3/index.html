Lesson 3 of 5

Research, cover letters, and how to apply

Job Bank says applying for a job involves three steps: research the company or job, create a resume, and prepare a cover letter.

Research first. Before you send anything, try to learn:

  • What the company does
  • The duties of the job
  • The qualifications and skills required
  • Who the customers or clients are
  • What reputation the employer has

Job Bank says this research helps you write the resume and cover letter and prepare for an interview. You can use the posting, the company’s website, and (if it feels safe) people who work in that field.

A cover letter is your introduction. Job Bank says it should be concise, well written, and tailored to that company and job. You may need a different letter for each job. It should give the employer an idea of who you are and highlight the skills you offer.

On its “stand out” page, Job Bank suggests:

  • a short opening that shows interest
  • your top skills that match the posting, with one or two clear examples
  • a closing that repeats your interest or thanks the reader

A cover letter is not always required, but when it is, Job Bank says taking time to write a good one can make a real difference.

When you apply: follow the instructions on the posting exactly (email, website form, or in person). Attach the files they ask for. Keep a copy of what you sent and the date.

If a posting feels like a scam (for example, it asks for a banking password or a fee to “guarantee” a job), stop and ask council staff. See also the Range Path email-safety course.
