Formulas that start with =

About 12 min

A formula tells Excel to calculate. Microsoft says a simple formula always starts with an equal sign (=). Then you type numbers and operators:

  • plus + to add
  • minus - to subtract
  • asterisk * to multiply
  • forward slash / to divide

Examples from Microsoft: =2+4, =4-2, =2*4, or =4/2. Click a cell, type the formula, then press Enter (Windows) or Return (Mac). You can also point at cells instead of typing the numbers: for example, =B2+B3+B4.

Microsoft notes that multiplication and division happen before addition and subtraction. So =5+2*3 multiplies 2 and 3 first, then adds 5.

Worked example: order of operations

Problem

What does the formula =5+2*3 calculate?

Steps

  1. Start the formula with an equal sign.
  2. Remember that multiplication happens before addition.
  3. Multiply 2 by 3 first, then add 5.

Result

The lesson states that =5+2*3 multiplies 2 and 3 first, then adds 5.

AutoSum

About 10 min

AutoSum is a fast way to add a column or row. Microsoft’s steps:

  1. Select the cell immediately below a column of numbers, or immediately to the right of a row of numbers.
  2. On the Home tab (or the Formulas tab), click AutoSum.
  3. Press Enter.

AutoSum inserts a SUM function for you. You can also type a SUM formula yourself, such as =SUM(B2:B4), which adds the cells from B2 through B4.

If a formula does not look right, click the cell and check the formula bar at the top. Fix the equal sign and cell addresses, then press Enter again.

Practise on the sample amounts from Lesson 2. This is not payroll or tax calculation advice.

Worked example: add a column

Problem

Add the sample amounts 80, 45, and 25.

Steps

  1. Select the cell immediately below the column of numbers.
  2. On the Home tab, click AutoSum.
  3. Or type =SUM(B2:B4) if those amounts are in B2 through B4.
  4. Press Enter.

Result

Excel adds the cells from B2 through B4.
