Overtime hours and overtime pay

Hours of work matter for what you are owed. Daily maximums, rest breaks, and days of rest are set out on Alberta’s “Hours of work and rest” page. Another Range Path course already maps those rest-and-break rules for people who supervise. This lesson does not repeat that list. If you need breaks or a weekly day of rest, read the live hours page.

This lesson covers overtime, which that other course did not teach.

As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta’s overtime page said overtime is all hours worked over 8 hours a day or 44 hours a week, whichever is greater. That is called the 8/44 rule. Most employees, including people paid a salary, are entitled to overtime pay. There are exemptions for some industries and professions — check the live page rather than guessing.

Except where there is a written overtime agreement, an employer must pay overtime of at least 1.5 times your regular wage rate for overtime hours. Instead of paying that money, an employer may give time off with pay (banked overtime) at a rate of at least one hour off for each overtime hour, but only as part of an overtime agreement. Banked time must usually be used within 6 months of the end of the pay period in which you earned it, unless a collective agreement says otherwise.

Employers and employees cannot make an agreement that pays less than the Code’s minimum overtime. If your workplace uses an averaging arrangement or is in an industry with different overtime rules, read those pages before you do the math.
