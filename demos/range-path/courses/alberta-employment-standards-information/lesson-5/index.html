How to make an employment standards complaint

If you think your employer is not following the Employment Standards Code, you can make a complaint. Talk to your employer first. Alberta says that is the first step in trying to resolve the issue.

You may complain while you are still employed, or at any time up to 6 months after your last day of employment. Complaints can be submitted online at no cost. They must be truthful. Your employer cannot terminate your employment because you make, or are about to make, a complaint.

A formal complaint can lead to pay you are owed or to other issues being resolved. Your name will be associated with the complaint and shared with the employer. That is different from an anonymous tip, which does not use your name and will not update you on the result.

Employment Standards can look at issues the Code covers, such as wages, overtime, minimum wage, vacation, general holiday pay, pay statements, unauthorized deductions, hours of work and rest, job-protected leaves, and termination. It does not handle Records of Employment, Employment Insurance, occupational health and safety, WCB claims, human-rights complaints, or tax deductions. Those belong to other offices.

Before you start, wait one pay period if the issue is about wages or other earnings, in case the next pay statement fixes it. Waiting is not required for urgent matters such as job-protected leaves. Gather documents that help, such as your wage, the employer’s address, two ways to contact you, timesheets, and your employment contract if you have one.

You need an Alberta.ca Account to submit the online complaint. Work must have been performed in Alberta, in a provincially regulated industry, by an employee (not a contractor). If you are in a union, issues are often handled through a grievance, not this complaint.
