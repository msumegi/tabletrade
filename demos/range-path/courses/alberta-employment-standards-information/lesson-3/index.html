Vacation and general holidays

Most employees — full-time and part-time — are entitled to vacation time and vacation pay. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta’s vacation page said you must usually work for one year before you are entitled to vacation time. After that, the minimums are:

  • 2 weeks with pay after each of the first 4 years of employment
  • 3 weeks with pay after 5 consecutive years of employment

For people paid hourly, weekly, or by commission, vacation pay is generally 4% of wages for the 2-week entitlement and 6% after 5 years. If employment ends before 12 months, the employer must pay 4% of the wages you earned. Time on a job-protected leave counts toward your length of employment for vacation.

Employers must give vacation, and employees must take it. It should be one unbroken period unless you ask in writing to split it. If you cannot agree on dates, the employer can decide, but must give at least 2 weeks’ written notice of the start date.

Alberta has 9 general holidays, also called statutory holidays: New Year’s Day, Alberta Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Remembrance Day, and Christmas Day. Employers may choose to recognize extra days, such as Heritage Day or the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation.

You are generally eligible for general holiday pay if you have worked for the same employer for at least 30 workdays in the 12 months before the holiday. What you are owed then depends on whether the holiday is a regular day of work for you and whether you work that day. The official page explains those calculations. This course does not walk through every formula.
