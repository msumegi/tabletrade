Getting paid at least the minimum

Alberta’s Employment Standards Code sets minimum standards that employers must provide to employees. This lesson is for people who are paid to work, not for people who are writing payroll rules.

As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta’s minimum-wage page said employers must pay at least the minimum wage. The general minimum wage for most employees was $15 an hour. A lower student rate of $13 an hour can apply to students under 18, with restrictions. Separate weekly or monthly minimums apply to some salespersons and to some domestic employees. Wages do not include tips or expense money.

Employers must also follow payment-of-earnings rules. They must pay at least once a month, or on a daily, weekly, bi-weekly, or semi-monthly pay period. They must pay you within 10 consecutive days after the end of the pay period, unless employment has ended. You must be paid in Canadian currency, by cash, cheque, or direct deposit into an account of your choice. You are not considered paid until you have received the funds.

At the end of each pay period, your employer must give you a statement of earnings. That statement should show the period, regular and overtime hours, wage and overtime rates, each kind of earning listed separately (such as wages, overtime, general holiday pay, and vacation pay), deductions and the reason for each one, and any hours taken off instead of overtime.

These figures can change. Check the live pages before you rely on a number.
