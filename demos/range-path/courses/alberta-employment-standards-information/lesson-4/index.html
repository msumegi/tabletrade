Job-protected leaves and ending employment

Job-protected leaves give you time off to deal with life events without losing your job. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta said employees are eligible for most of these leaves after 90 days with the same employer. The leaves are unpaid, though Employment Insurance may provide income support in some cases.

Alberta lists leaves such as bereavement, compassionate care, critical illness, domestic violence, long-term illness and injury, maternity and parental leave, personal and family responsibility, and others. Each leave has its own length and notice rules. Read the page for the leave that applies to you before you give notice.

Ending a job also has written-notice rules. Notice is not required if employment is 90 days or less. If you resign after that, you must usually give 1 week of written notice if you have worked less than 2 years, or 2 weeks if you have worked 2 years or more.

If the employer ends the job — and it is not just cause or another listed exception — written notice grows with your service: 1 week (more than 90 days but less than 2 years), 2 weeks (2 to less than 4 years), 4 weeks (4 to less than 6), 5 weeks (6 to less than 8), 6 weeks (8 to less than 10), or 8 weeks (10 years or more). The employer may pay termination pay instead of notice, or use a mix of both.

During a notice period, the employer cannot reduce your earnings or require you to use vacation or banked overtime. Final earnings must be paid within 10 calendar days after the end of the pay period in which the job ended, or 31 calendar days after your last day. This course does not advise anyone on dismissing a person or on common-law severance.
