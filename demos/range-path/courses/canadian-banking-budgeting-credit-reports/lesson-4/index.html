Making a simple budget

FCAC says a budget is a plan that helps you manage your money. It helps you see how much money you get, spend, and save. A budget can help you balance income with savings and expenses and guide spending toward your goals.

Before you build one, FCAC suggests you think about short-term and long-term goals and include saving for those goals. It also suggests creating an emergency fund that could cover living expenses for 3 to 6 months, while noting that amount can feel out of reach — so start with a small amount on a regular basis. That is general guidance from FCAC, not a personal plan for your household.

Next, know where your money is going. Try tracking what you spend for one or two months, including bills. FCAC suggests dividing expenses into needs and wants. A need is necessary, such as housing, clothing, food, or medication. A want is something you would like but do not necessarily need, such as restaurant meals. Needs and wants are not the same for everyone and can change over time.

FCAC’s Budget Planner is a free official tool. You enter income, savings, and expenses, review results, and save the budget online. The tool may compare your categories with average ranges and suggest next steps. Use it on the making-a-budget page. Tips for staying with a budget include keeping receipts, updating the plan when income or bills change, and comparing the plan with what you actually spent at the end of the month.

This lesson is not tax advice, not lending advice, and not a copy of any community workbook. If numbers feel hard, the Range Path everyday-math course practises numeracy separately.
