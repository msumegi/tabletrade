Credit reports and credit scores

Your credit report is a summary of how you have used credit in the past. Credit bureaus create it when you first borrow money or apply for credit. It can include credit cards, loans, and mortgages, how much you owe, and whether you pay on time. Lenders use the report, and FCAC notes it can also affect renting a home.

Your credit score is a three-digit number from your credit report. It is meant to show how likely you are to repay borrowed money. As of November 2026, FCAC said scores usually range from 300 to 900, and a higher score is better. Your score can rise when you pay bills on time and use credit carefully, and fall when you miss payments or carry too much debt. Exact formulas are not public.

Canada’s two main credit bureaus are Equifax and TransUnion. They collect credit activity in Canada. You can access your credit report online for free from both. You can also request a free copy by mail, or in person in some provinces, using the steps on FCAC’s page. Checking your own report or score does not affect your credit score.

As of November 2026, FCAC said the Equifax score is free online in all provinces and territories. A TransUnion score was free if you live in Quebec or Ontario (as part of your Consumer Disclosure). Alberta learners should read the live page before assuming both scores are free.

Fraudsters may pretend to offer free reports. Use official bureau sites linked from FCAC, look for https, and do not give personal details to a company you have not checked.

If you find a mistake, FCAC says you can ask the bureau to correct it. This course does not repair your credit or settle debts.
