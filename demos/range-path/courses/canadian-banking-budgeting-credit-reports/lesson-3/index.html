Low-cost and no-cost accounts

Some financial institutions offer low-cost and no-cost accounts with basic features.

As of November 2026, FCAC’s page said a low-cost account has a monthly fee of $4 or less. The institution cannot require you to keep a minimum balance in that kind of account. Low-cost accounts are available to all Canadians. Some other accounts may also cost $4 or less without meeting the official low-cost definition — ask the institution.

A no-cost account has the same features and services as low-cost accounts, but no monthly fee. FCAC listed these eligibility groups: 18 years old or younger; a student; a senior who receives the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS); a Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP) beneficiary; and a newcomer to Canada (eligible for the first year in Canada). The page also said you may qualify if you are Indigenous, a recipient of the Disability Tax Credit, or a recipient of social assistance payments from select provincial or territorial programs. Check the live page; these groups can change.

Low-cost and no-cost accounts include at least 18 debit transactions per month. Confirm with the institution which transactions count. FCAC also says these accounts must include other features at no extra cost, such as free digital monthly statements or cheque-writing privileges.

Use FCAC’s Account Comparison Tool, linked from that page, instead of guessing. Look at your total yearly costs, including ATM, overdraft, NSF, and foreign-transaction fees — not only the monthly fee.

This course does not pick an institution for you.
