Words for kinds of care

Official Alberta pages use several care words. Learning them helps you choose the right door — without this course choosing it for you.

Primary care is described by Primary Care Alberta as the first step for most health concerns. It includes family doctors, nurse practitioners, and other providers who help with everyday needs such as checkups, advice when you are sick or injured, prescriptions and refills, long-term conditions, mental health concerns, and referrals for specialists, tests, or other services.

Alberta Find a Provider says:

  • A family doctor provides ongoing, complete care for people of all ages, and supports specialist or hospital care when needed.
  • A nurse practitioner can assess, diagnose, treat illnesses, prescribe medications, and provide ongoing care.
  • Walk-in clinics provide non-emergency medical care without an appointment for minor illnesses, injuries, and general health.

Diagnose, on that official description, is part of what a nurse practitioner can do: figure out an illness so it can be treated. Treat means providing care for the illness. Prescribe means authorizing a medication. This course still cannot diagnose or treat you.

Alberta’s Know Your Options page says to call 911 for a medical emergency. If you are unsure where to go, call 811 for advice on your symptoms (what you feel or notice) and care options.

These words help you understand a poster or a receptionist. They do not replace a clinician.
