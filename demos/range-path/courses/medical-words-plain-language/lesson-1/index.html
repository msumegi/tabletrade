Why health words matter

Health information is easier to use when the words are clear. Health Canada and the Public Health Agency of Canada publish a series called It’s Your Health: more than 150 easy-to-understand articles to help people make informed decisions about health and safety. That series is a place to read official, plain-language articles. It is not a diagnosis of your own health.

This course teaches words you may hear at a clinic or see on a medicine label. It does not train you to work in a medical office, code a bill, or give care to someone else.

Health Canada’s page on using medications safely says prescription and non-prescription medications can help treat diseases and conditions, but taking them correctly is important. All medications carry some risk. The page uses the words side effects (also called adverse reactions) for unwanted effects that can happen even when a product is used correctly. Examples it gives include a mild rash or a severe allergic reaction.

If a provider uses a word you do not know, ask them to say it in everyday language. Write the word down. You can also call 811 in Alberta for nurse advice, or 911 in a medical emergency. Those numbers come from official Alberta pages, not from this course guessing what you should do.

Do not use random websites or social media posts as your only source. Lesson 5 points to official Canadian pages.
