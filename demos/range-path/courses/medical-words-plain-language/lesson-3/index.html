Words on a medicine label

Health Canada talks about both prescription medications (you get them with a prescriber’s order) and non-prescription medications (you can buy them without that order). Always use medication as directed by your doctor or pharmacist: when to take it, how often, and for how long. Always read the product label.

A dose is how much you take at one time. Health Canada says you should also know what to do if you miss a dose, and when you should stop a medication. Do not guess. Ask the pharmacist.

A side effect or adverse reaction is an unwanted effect. It can be mild or severe. Health Canada says to report a side effect to your healthcare professional, and you can also report it to Health Canada.

Tell your provider about allergies and sensitivities, your other health conditions, and all medications you take, including natural health products such as vitamins and herbal remedies. An interaction is when a medication does not mix safely with certain foods, drinks, vitamins, or other products. Health Canada gives grapefruit juice as one example of a drink to ask about.

Keep medicines in their original containers, out of the reach of children, and return expired medicine to a pharmacy or local disposal depot. If you think someone has taken too much, get emergency help — do not use this course as poison-control advice. MyHealth Alberta lists the Poison and Drug Information Service (PADIS) at 1-800-332-1414, and 911 for emergencies.
