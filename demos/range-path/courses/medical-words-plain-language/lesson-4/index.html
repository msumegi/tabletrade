Lesson 4 of 5

Questions to ask, and a simple medicine list

Clear questions protect you. Health Canada’s “using medications safely” page says to talk with your doctor, pharmacist, or other healthcare professional about all aspects of your medication. Ask:

  • why you are being prescribed the medication
  • how it should work
  • whether you will need regular check-ups or tests
  • how to reduce the risk of side effects and interactions with food, drinks, vitamins, or herbal supplements
  • when you should expect to start feeling better
  • if and when you should go back to your doctor
  • what to do if you miss a dose

Ask your pharmacist for written information about the medications you take. Health Canada authorizes a Patient Medication Information section in a drug’s product monograph, written in plain language so it is easier to understand. A one-page pharmacy printout is not always the same as that authorized information, so ask if you want the official patient information.

Health Canada offers a medication chart to track:

  • the name (brand name and ingredients)
  • the dosage
  • the time you take it
  • the reason you take it
  • side effects you notice

Include prescription medicines, non-prescription medicines, and natural health products. Share the list with your doctor and pharmacist. Using the same pharmacy, Health Canada says, helps the pharmacist see possible harmful interactions.

Mention if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning a pregnancy, and mention trouble swallowing pills or remembering doses. This is so your provider can advise you — not so this course can.
