Lesson 3 of 5

Make a simple study plan that fits adult life

A study plan is a habit, not a promise that you will pass. Canada’s Skills for Success include adaptability: the ability to set or adjust goals, plan and prioritize, stay focused, and keep going when something is hard.

Useful adaptability habits from the official skill list include:

  • set a goal that matches the time and support you have
  • break the goal into tasks and put them in order
  • manage your time (for example, being on time and not wasting the time you set aside)
  • stay focused on the task in front of you
  • keep trying when a plan does not work, or change the plan if you need to

Because the official CAEC page says you can move at your own pace, you do not have to study every subject at once. A simple plan might look like this:

  1. Open the official test outcomes for one subject.
  2. Choose two or three short study times in the week that fit around work and family.
  3. Write those times down where you will see them.
  4. At each session, do one small task, such as reading a short text or trying part of an official sample test.
  5. After a week, look at what worked and adjust the plan.

If your council also offers the Range Path course “How to Learn Online,” you can use it for more study-habit ideas. This CAEC course does not repeat that whole course.

A plan you can keep for 20 minutes, three times a week, is more useful than a perfect timetable you never follow. This is practice, not a guarantee.
