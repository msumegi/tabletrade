Get help from official places when you get stuck

Getting stuck is part of learning. The next step is to use official help, not to guess or to buy an unofficial “pass guaranteed” course.

The how-to page says some testing centres offer a test preparation class. Visit your province or territory’s website to find a testing centre near you. If you need special arrangements or accommodations, request them from your province or territory. The overview page says the CAEC is meant to support test-takers who need access to learning accommodations.

For questions about the CAEC website, test format, content, rules, and information, Alberta lists these emails (as of 10 November 2026, America/Regina):

  • General inquiries: edc.caec@gov.ab.ca
  • Technical support: ecc.caec@gov.ab.ca

A local CALP program, tutor, or adult-learning centre can also help you build reading, writing, and math habits. They cannot issue the CAEC. Only the official process does that after you pass the required tests.

When you are ready to register, go back to the official Alberta pages. Check eligibility, create your account on the CAEC digital platform, and book with a testing centre. Bring the identification the official page asks for, and look up fees on the live provincial information. This course does not quote fees and does not walk you through every screen.

Remember what this course is. It supports study habits. It is not the exam, not a pass promise, and not the credential itself.
