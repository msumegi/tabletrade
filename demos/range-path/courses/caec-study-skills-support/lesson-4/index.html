Lesson 4 of 5

Practise reading, writing, and numeracy habits

The official how-to page asks you to review test outcomes and practise with a sample test. Those tools show you what each subject covers. This lesson is about everyday habits that support the same skills. It is not a set of exam tricks, and it does not include real or copied test questions.

Skills for Success describes reading as finding, understanding, and using information in words, symbols, and images. Helpful habits include knowing why you are reading, scanning or skimming to find key information, and saying the main idea in your own words.

Writing is sharing information with written words, symbols, and images. Helpful habits include knowing your purpose and your reader, planning what to say, writing in clear sentences, and reading your work again to fix meaning and spelling.

Numeracy is finding, understanding, using, and reporting mathematical information in words, numbers, symbols, and graphics. Helpful habits include deciding what question you need to answer, finding the numbers that matter, and checking whether your result makes sense.

You can practise these habits in daily life in Alberta: read a short official page or community notice and tell someone the main point; write a clear note or email; work with numbers on a receipt or a simple measurement. When you are ready, try an official sample test from the how-to page. Use it to see the format, not as proof that you will pass.

If a sample test feels hard, that is information for your plan. It does not mean you should stop. Ask a tutor or adult-learning program for help with the skill, and keep using official resources.
