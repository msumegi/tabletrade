What people mean by “AI”

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a name for computer systems that do tasks people often use thinking for, such as making sense of spoken language, learning from examples, or solving problems. A Government of Canada guide on generative AI uses that kind of plain description.

Generative AI is a type of AI that makes new content. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (Cyber Centre) says it creates unique content by learning from large sets of data that were fed into the model. That content can be text, images, audio, video, or computer code. Older, more traditional AI systems often recognize patterns or sort existing content. Generative AI can create something new.

You use generative AI by giving it a short instruction, called a prompt or query. The Government of Canada guide explains that many of these tools produce the next words or images that are statistically likely, based on their training data. The result can look as though a person made it. That does not mean a person checked it, and it does not mean it is true.

People use these tools for everyday things such as drafting a note, summarizing a long page, or making an image. Organizations also use AI in many kinds of work. This course does not say you must use AI, and it does not name or recommend a product.

This course is not a technical certificate. It will not teach you how to build an AI system. The next lessons focus on three practical skills: checking answers, protecting your privacy, and watching for scams.
