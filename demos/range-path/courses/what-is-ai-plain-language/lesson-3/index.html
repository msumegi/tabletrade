Protect your privacy with AI chatbots

An AI chatbot is a tool that answers in writing (or sometimes by voice) after you type or speak a prompt. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) says the information you put into a chatbot may be collected and stored. Put your privacy first.

The OPC’s key tips are:

  • Limit the personal information you share about yourself.
  • Do not share sensitive or identifiable personal information.
  • Change personal details if you must give an example — for instance, use a made-up name and change anything that could identify a real person.
  • If the chatbot allows it, consider deleting your chat history.
  • Review and adjust your privacy settings, and use strong passwords.
  • Do not share personal information about other people.
  • Never share pictures or personal information of minors (children and young people under 18).

The OPC also lists risks. The information may be stored. The company may not clearly explain how it is used, stored, or shared. It may be used to train the AI system further. It may be used to create profiles about you or to make decisions about you. Future uses may not be clear. A chatbot could even disclose information it has learned about you to other users.

The Cyber Centre adds a similar warning: people may put sensitive personal information into AI prompts without thinking. Threat actors could use that information to impersonate someone or spread false information. Do not share private information with an AI tool unless you understand what it does with your data. Avoid putting personally identifiable information into prompts. See whether the tool lets you delete your prompt history.

This course does not tell you which chatbot to use. If you do use one, share as little as you can.
