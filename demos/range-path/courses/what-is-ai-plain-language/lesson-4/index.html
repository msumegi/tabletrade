AI can make scams look more real

Threat actors use AI in scams. The Cyber Centre says content that is not clearly labelled as AI-generated can spread false information and confusion. AI also helps criminals write phishing messages more often, more automatically, and in a more polished way. A realistic scam message can lead to identity theft, financial fraud, or other cybercrime.

Get Cyber Safe says phishing is still a common way criminals trick people. AI can make those messages look more legitimate, with fewer of the spelling mistakes people used to watch for. It can also use details from social media to make a message feel personal. Red flags that still matter include requests for money, suspicious links or attachments, and email addresses or web names that do not look right.

Get Cyber Safe also describes vishing, or voice phishing. AI can copy the sound of a real person’s voice, including a family member or friend. The page says more of these calls have been reported in Canada, including calls that pretend a loved one is in trouble and needs money or information right away. Stay calm. If anyone calls with an urgent demand for money or personal information, check who they are through another method — for example, hang up and call the person or the organization using a number you already trust. Get Cyber Safe says no law enforcement or government organization in Canada will demand money or sensitive information over the phone. Your bank will not email or call to ask for your password, one-time passcode, card number, or similar secrets.

AI can also create new images and videos that do not exist anywhere else, so an old “search for this picture” check may not prove they are real. If something seems too good to be true, it probably is.
