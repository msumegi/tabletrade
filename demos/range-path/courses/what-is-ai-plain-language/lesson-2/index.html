Check AI answers before you use them

Generative AI can sound confident even when it is wrong. The Cyber Centre says you should be cautious with information that comes from AI. Its outputs:

  • can be incorrect
  • might not make sense
  • might not take certain factors into account
  • can be biased

The Cyber Centre also says you should analyze AI content before you act on it or use it, and you should validate your sources to check whether the content is accurate.

A Government of Canada guide on generative AI gives the same kind of warning. These tools can produce content that looks well developed and reasonable but is inaccurate, incomplete, or not consistent with the facts. The guide calls some of that content a hallucination. Training data can also be out of date or leave out many views, so the answer may be biased or missing important context.

Useful habits from those official pages:

  1. Do not treat an AI answer as the final word.
  2. Check important facts against a trusted source, such as an official government page, your council, your school, or another source you already trust.
  3. If the tool does not show sources you can open and check, do not use it as if it were a search engine.
  4. If you cannot tell whether the answer is right, do not use it for a decision that matters — money, health, legal questions, or someone else’s rights.

Get Cyber Safe notes that AI tools often present their content as true facts even when it is not. That can lead people to spread false information without meaning to. Pause, check, and then decide.

This course cannot check an answer for you. You stay responsible for what you send, post, or act on.
