Everyday habits that help you stay safer

You do not need to become a cyber-security expert. Official Canadian pages point to simple habits.

The Cyber Centre says individuals should practise basic cyber security hygiene: stay informed, use strong passwords, turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA), keep software up to date, use antivirus software, and avoid public Wi-Fi networks. It also suggests posting less personal information online, not opening attachments or links from unknown sources, and using another verified way to communicate if something feels off.

When you look at pictures, videos, or text, Get Cyber Safe lists signs that content may have been made by AI:

  • Images: missing or misplaced objects or body parts (especially hands), odd shadows or lighting, text that does not make sense, or skin and fabric that look unusually smooth or glossy. Some tools add a watermark or small logo.
  • Videos: unnatural movement, people who do not blink, strange shadows, blurs, or light flickers, and voices that sound flat, choppy, or paired with odd background noise.
  • Text: a dry, “robotic” tone, information that is out of date, or the same words repeated again and again.

These signs are clues, not proof. AI is changing, and some fakes will be hard to spot. Always check the source. If a message asks you to send money, click a link, or share personal information, pause.

Do not treat this course as approval to use any one AI product. Check answers, protect privacy, and verify unexpected requests. If you want more practice on email and passwords, your council may also offer the Range Path course on email and online safety.
