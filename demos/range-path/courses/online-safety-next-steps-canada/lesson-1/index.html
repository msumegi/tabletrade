Add a second lock: multi-factor authentication

A password or passphrase is important, but Get Cyber Safe says even the strongest password cannot be 100% foolproof. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds an extra step when you sign in. If a cyber criminal ever gets your password, another defence is in place to stop them.

Get Cyber Safe says MFA is more secure because it focuses on things that are unique to you:

  • What you know: a PIN, password, or security question
  • What you have: your phone or a fob
  • What you are: your fingerprint, your face, your eyes, or your voice

The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security gives the same three kinds of factors. It says two or more different factors are needed to unlock a device or sign in. Something you know can be easy to compromise, which is why the Cyber Centre strongly recommends adding another factor when you can.

Two-factor authentication (2FA) is a type of MFA that uses two different kinds of factors. A code sent to your phone is an example of “something you have.” A fingerprint is “something you are.”

Most social media sites and many devices offer MFA in their security settings. Get Cyber Safe’s advice is simple: whenever a website, app, or device allows you to enable MFA, turn it on.

How to write a strong passphrase is taught in other Range Path courses. This lesson asks you to add the extra lock, not to rebuild the first one.

MFA helps a great deal, but it is not magic. The important habit is to switch it on for the accounts that matter most, such as email, cloud storage, and banking, when the service offers it.
