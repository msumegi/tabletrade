Kids’ devices and parental controls

Kids use the internet for schoolwork, socializing, and gaming. Get Cyber Safe offers a checklist you can go through with the children in your life. You do not have to be a technical expert. You do need to talk with them.

The official checklist includes these steps:

  1. Make a list of all the devices your kids use that connect to the internet.
  2. Make a list of all the accounts they use, including social media or gaming, and family accounts such as streaming services.
  3. Use a unique passphrase for each account. Other courses explain how to build a passphrase. Help the family keep those sign-ins safe. If you use a password manager, protect it with your strongest passphrase.
  4. Turn on parental controls for any shared accounts the children can access, so they are not watching content or using features you do not want them to.
  5. Enable MFA whenever it is available. When your child signs in, they may be asked for another factor such as a fingerprint or a text-message code.

Get Cyber Safe also says to set a good example on your own devices, and to go through a kids’ cyber-security checklist together. Let them take the lead where they can. They may even teach you something.

Tell the children they can come to you if they see something online they do not understand. The official page mentions that some threats, such as phishing, happen all over the internet. Spotting scam messages is covered in another Range Path course, so this lesson does not repeat those signs.

This is general information for families, not legal advice about parenting or about a child’s accounts.
