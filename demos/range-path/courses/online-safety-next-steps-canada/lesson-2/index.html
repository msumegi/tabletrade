Recover an account if something goes wrong

Get Cyber Safe says any of your online accounts can be a target. Signs that an account may have been compromised include being logged out unexpectedly, or security notices about login attempts from strange locations or unusual times.

If that happens, Get Cyber Safe lists four steps.

Step 1. Try to get back in through the account’s recovery page. That page is usually found under “need help” or “settings.” Answer the security questions that are there to help you recover the account.

Step 2. Contact the provider of the account. Do this if you have lost access — for example after too many wrong password attempts — or if you suspect a cyber criminal has the account. The provider may be able to tell you more and to track other suspicious behaviour.

Step 3. Change the passphrase or password. Use a strong, unique one. Other Range Path courses explain how to build a passphrase of four or more random words. This lesson does not repeat that recipe. The point here is to change it after a scare, and not to reuse an old one.

Step 4. Enable MFA, as you practised in Lesson 1. Get Cyber Safe says MFA helps protect your information even if your password has been compromised.

The Cyber Centre also says you should have a clear recovery plan for a lost or compromised extra factor — for example if you lose the phone you use for codes. Know, before you need it, how that service lets you recover access.

Recovering an account is stressful. Follow the official steps rather than clicking a random “we locked your account” message. Messages like that belong to other safety courses about scams.
