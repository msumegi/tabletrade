What numeracy is

Numeracy is more than school math. The Government of Canada’s Skills for Success model defines it as the ability to find, understand, use, and report mathematical information presented through words, numbers, symbols, and graphics. Official examples include performing calculations, making estimations, and making sense of numbers you see in daily life.

The official skill has clear steps:

  1. Know what question you need to answer.
  2. Find the useful numbers (you may need to scan a bill, a label, or a table).
  3. Connect the pieces of information.
  4. Calculate, put things in order, measure, or estimate.
  5. Check whether the result makes sense.
  6. Share the result if someone else needs it — in writing, in a table, or by speaking.

A Government of Canada numeracy self-assessment (written for the earlier Essential Skills framework, and still posted) lists everyday tasks such as adding and subtracting, making change, adding up a bill, taking simple measurements, comparing sizes or amounts, recording time, estimating quantities, calculating percentages, and comparing similar products to see which is better value.

This course practises those number skills. It does not tell you how to manage debt, choose a loan, or make a household budget, even though some official lists mention budgets as a numeracy task. For money-product rules, use official FCAC pages later or ask a qualified person.

Bring a calculator if it helps — knowing which numbers to use still matters.
