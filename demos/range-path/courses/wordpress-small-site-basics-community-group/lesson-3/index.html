Lesson 3 of 5

Pages stay; posts are dated news

In WordPress you can add content as a Post or a Page. They both have a title and content, but they are used differently.

WordPress.org says a Post is a regular blog entry. In a default setup, posts appear in reverse chronological order on the blog’s home page. Use posts for news, a monthly update, or “photos from Saturday’s supper.”

A Page is for content that is not tied to a date in the same way. Pages live outside the normal blog timeline. They often present information that stays relevant, such as About or Contact. You can add as many pages as you like and update them whenever you need to. You can even have a WordPress site that uses only pages.

To create a page:

  1. Log in to the admin screen.
  2. Click Pages > Add New in the left sidebar (or use Add New on the Pages screen).
  3. Add a title.
  4. Add the body content.
  5. When the page is ready, click Publish.

You can organize pages into parent pages and subpages. For example, an About page could have a subpage for the board or for meeting times. You do not have to use subpages on a very small site.

After you publish a page, visitors still need a way to find it. The next lessons cover writing with blocks and adding the page to a menu.
