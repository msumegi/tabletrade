An appointment is not the same as a meeting

About 3 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

Microsoft’s basic Outlook page (opened 10 November 2026) treats appointments and meetings as different things, even though both live on your calendar.

An appointment is time you put on your own calendar that does not invite other people and does not reserve a resource such as a conference room or equipment. A quiet half hour to review Saturday market notes is an appointment. No one else needs an invitation. No room needs to be booked.

A meeting includes other people and can include resources such as conference rooms. A fifteen-minute briefing that asks Jordan to join you is a meeting. Microsoft says you will get responses to your meeting requests in your Inbox — Accept, Decline, or a similar reply, depending on what the other person chooses.

That difference saves embarrassment. If you use New Appointment for a briefing and never invite Jordan, Jordan will not know to come. If you use New Meeting for a private review hour and add half the building, you have turned quiet work time into a group event.

Switch to Calendar using the navigation you learned in Lesson 1. The calendar grid shows days and times. Screens differ in classic Outlook, new Outlook, the web, and mobile apps. The two commands you need today still have the same idea: New Appointment for your own time, and New Meeting when other people are part of the plan.

You do not need every calendar tool Microsoft offers. This lesson does not teach repeating events, room finders, or shared workplace calendars that your organization may manage differently. If staff use a shared calendar for vacations or hall bookings, follow their instructions. The calendar planning sheet for this course keeps you with one appointment and one simple meeting.

Create an appointment

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

In a Calendar folder, Microsoft says to choose New Appointment. You can also right-click a time block on the calendar grid and choose New Appointment. The keyboard shortcut Microsoft lists is Ctrl+Shift+A.

Give the appointment a clear title, the way you give an email a clear subject. “Review Saturday market notes” is more useful later than “Busy.” Set the date, the start time, and the end time. The practice sheet uses Wednesday, 12 November 2026, from 1:00 p.m. to 1:30 p.m. If you are working in a live practice account after that date, pick the next free half hour instead. Do not invite anyone and do not book a room. That is what makes it an appointment.

Set a reminder so the time does not slip by. Microsoft says to open the appointment (or meeting) and, in the Reminder list, choose how long before the event you want the reminder to appear. To turn a reminder off, choose None. The practice sheet suggests 15 minutes before.

Then save the item with the save control on your screen. The label can differ by version, so look for a save command rather than Send — you are not sending this appointment to other people. If you cannot find save, ask a tutor. After you save, you should see the title on the calendar grid at the right time.

If you created the item from the wrong button and it looks like a meeting, close it without sending an invite and start again with New Appointment. It is easier to begin once more than to send a meeting request you did not mean to send.

Create a simple meeting

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

When other people need to be there, choose New Meeting from a Calendar folder. Microsoft lists the shortcut Ctrl+Shift+Q, which creates a new meeting request from any folder in Outlook — even from Mail. That is handy if you are reading a thread and realize the group needs a short conversation instead of another long email. Microsoft’s writing-advice page said the same idea in reverse: after many messages without a resolution, consider a meeting.

Add a clear title, a date, and start and end times. The practice sheet uses “Saturday market briefing” on Thursday, 13 November 2026, from 10:00 a.m. to 10:15 a.m. In the invite field, add the practice name Jordan Lee and the sample address from the sheet. Write one line in the body, such as “Confirm who is bringing extra chairs.” Set a reminder if you want one.

Then send the meeting request. Unlike an appointment, a meeting is meant to reach other people. Microsoft says replies arrive in your Inbox. In a tutor-approved practice account, you may not receive a real reply from Jordan — the address is sample only. The habit still matters: after you send a real meeting at work, watch the Inbox for responses instead of assuming everyone saw the invite.

Do not invite real coworkers from this course unless your tutor says the practice account is safe to use that way. Never type a real workplace password into the course. If your workplace uses extra meeting tools (a video link, a room list, or a required form), follow those workplace rules. This lesson stays with the basic Microsoft distinction: appointment for you, meeting for you and other people.

Appointment: Review Saturday market notes

A coworker needs to join a short briefing, and you also want a private half hour to review your notes. Put the work in a sensible order.

  1. Decide which block of time is only for you, and which block needs another person
  2. Create an appointment (New Appointment) for your private review time
  3. Create a meeting (New Meeting), invite the other person, and send the request
  4. Watch your Inbox later for replies to the meeting request

Worked example 2. A short briefing with Jordan

Problem

Alex needs Jordan to confirm who is bringing chairs.

Steps

  1. That requires another person, so Alex chooses New Meeting , titles it “Saturday market briefing,” sets Thursday from 10:00 a.m.
  2. to 10:15 a.m., adds the practice address for Jordan Lee, writes one agenda line, and sends the request.
  3. Alex remembers that real replies would arrive in the Inbox.

Result

Because the address is sample only, Alex does not expect a live reply from a real coworker.

Try it

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

  1. Download the calendar planning sheet.
  2. In Calendar, create Plan 1 as an appointment (New Appointment or Ctrl+Shift+A). No guests and no room.
  3. Set a reminder, then save the appointment.
  4. Create Plan 2 as a meeting (New Meeting or Ctrl+Shift+Q). Add the practice invitee only if your tutor agrees.
  5. Write one sentence of agenda in the meeting body.
  6. Do not type a real workplace password into this course, and do not invite real people from a sample address unless staff say it is safe.

Discussion (optional)

About 2 minutes to read, plus a short practice.

If you are in a study group: name one weekly task that should be an appointment (only you) and one that should be a meeting (other people). Keep both examples sample-only.

Lesson 7 — Appointments and meetings

  • Appointment = your own time, no guests, no room. New Appointment or Ctrl+Shift+A.
  • Meeting = other people, and it can include a room. New Meeting or Ctrl+Shift+Q.
  • Give every item a clear title, date, start time, and end time.
  • Set a reminder from the Reminder list, or choose None.
  • Save an appointment. Send a meeting request.
  • Meeting replies arrive in your Inbox.
  • Use a practice account. Do not invite real people unless your tutor agrees.
