To, Cc, Bcc, and attachments

The address boxes tell Outlook who should get the message.

Microsoft’s “write great email” guidance says:

  • Put people who need to respond or take action on the To line.
  • Put people who need to be informed on the Cc line.
  • Limit the message to people who need to read it.

Bcc means blind carbon copy. When you put someone on the Bcc line, they receive a copy, but other recipients do not see that person’s name. In new Outlook, Microsoft’s steps include Options > Show fields > Show Bcc. In classic Outlook, you can choose Options > Bcc (or Bcc on the ribbon if you are writing in the reading pane).

Microsoft also warns: a Bcc recipient might reply to everyone by mistake and reveal they were copied. Do not use Bcc to secretly copy a manager on a sensitive note — Microsoft says it is safer to forward the sent message afterward if that person needs to know.

To attach a file (Microsoft):

  1. Create a message, or choose Reply, Reply All, or Forward.
  2. Choose Attach File (on the Message tab in some views).
  3. Pick the file, then Send.

To save an attachment you receive, select it and choose Save As (or right-click and Save As). Open unexpected attachments with care — see the LearnHere email-safety course if a file looks suspicious.

Never attach something you would not want a wider group to see.
