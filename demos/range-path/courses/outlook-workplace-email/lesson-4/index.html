Lesson 4 of 5

Write a clear, professional message

Knowing the buttons is only half the skill. Microsoft publishes “do’s and don’ts” for professional email in Outlook.

Do:

  • Read your message before you send it.
  • Write a descriptive, action-oriented subject, such as who it is for and what you need. Useful openings Microsoft mentions include “FYI:” and “Action Required.”
  • If the topic of a long conversation changes, change the subject.
  • Keep messages short and to the point. Put the most important information first.
  • Put questions or action items on their own lines so they stand out.
  • Use a signature when it fits, but keep it simple, short, and professional.
  • If you cannot give a full answer yet, send a short note that you are looking into it and will reply by a certain time.

Don’t (from the same Microsoft list):

  • Write in ALL CAPS.
  • Send a message while you are angry — better to save a draft and come back later.
  • Mark every message as High Importance; people will start to ignore it.
  • Use sarcasm, which is easy to misunderstand.
  • Write something you would not want others at work to read.
  • Reply All to a large group only to ask to be removed from a list.

These are workplace habits, not law. Your employer may have extra rules. When in doubt, keep the note short, polite, and clear.

If a problem needs a fast answer, Microsoft notes that a phone call or instant message can work better than a long email.
