Calendar, meetings, and your signature

Outlook’s Calendar helps you track your time. Microsoft makes a clear difference:

  • An appointment is something you put on your own calendar that does not invite other people or book a room. In a Calendar folder, choose New Appointment (or right-click a time and choose New Appointment).
  • A meeting includes other people and can include a room or other resources. Choose New Meeting. Replies to the meeting request arrive in your Inbox.

You can set a reminder so a notice appears before the appointment or meeting. Open the item and choose how much time before it you want the reminder — or choose None to turn it off.

To create a signature (Microsoft):

  1. In a new message, choose Signature > Signatures.
  2. On the Email Signature tab, choose New, type a name, and choose OK.
  3. You can set a default signature for new messages and, if you want, for replies and forwards.
  4. Under Edit signature, type the text and choose OK.

Microsoft’s professional-email guidance says to keep a signature simple, short, professional, and, if possible, free of large graphics.

A simple workplace signature might be your name, job title, and phone number — only details your workplace is comfortable sharing.

When you finish class, sign out if you are on a shared computer. Ask staff before you change settings on a learning-centre account.
