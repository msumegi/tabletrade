Cheat sheet

Sample steps only. Use the browser print dialog to save a PDF.

Lesson 1. Lesson 1 — Open Outlook

  • Start Outlook from the Start menu. Use a tutor-approved practice account.
  • Never type a real workplace password into this course.
  • Mail, Calendar, People, and Tasks move you among the main areas.
  • New messages usually arrive in the Inbox.
  • Switching to Calendar does not delete your mail.
  • Screens differ in classic, new, web, and mobile Outlook.
  • File > Add Account adds another account later — it is not how you open Calendar.

Lesson 2. Lesson 2 — Write and send

  • From the Inbox (or another mail folder), choose New Email. Shortcut: Ctrl+Shift+M.
  • Put the person who must act on the To line.
  • Write a descriptive, action-oriented subject (FYI: or Action Required can help).
  • Keep the body short. Most important point first. Questions on their own lines.
  • Read the message, then choose Send.
  • Check Sent Items rather than sending the same note twice.
  • Use a practice account. Never type a real workplace password into this course.

Lesson 3. Lesson 3 — Reply, Forward, To / Cc / Bcc

  • Reply = original sender only. Attachments not included.
  • Reply All = sender plus To and Cc. Attachments not included. Use it only when the group needs your words.
  • Forward = new recipients. Original attachments are included.
  • Subjects show RE: on a reply and FW: on a forward. Change the subject if the topic changes.
  • To = must act. Cc = needs to know. Bcc = receives a copy that others cannot see.
  • Show Bcc: new Outlook, Options > Show fields > Show Bcc; classic, Options > Bcc.
  • Do not secretly Bcc a manager on a sensitive note — forward the sent message instead.
  • Never Reply All only to ask to be removed from a large list.

Lesson 4. Lesson 4 — Attachments and tone

  • Message > Attach File to add a file from your computer.
  • Write one sentence that names the file and what the reader should do.
  • Save a received file with Save As (Attachments tab or right-click).
  • Do not open an unexpected attachment just to see what it is.
  • Signature > Signatures > New. Keep it simple, short, and free of large graphics.
  • Read before you send. Do not write in ALL CAPS or send while angry — save a draft.
  • Use High Importance sparingly. Do not write anything you would not want others at work to read.

Lesson 5. Lesson 5 — Folders, flags, and search

  • Classic Outlook: right-click in the Mail folder pane > New Folder. New Outlook: Create new folder.
  • File a message by dragging it onto the folder or by choosing Move.
  • Select the flag icon to make a to-do item (the flag turns red).
  • Right-click the flag > Add Reminder to pick a date and time.
  • Follow Up > Mark Complete when the follow-up is done.
  • Search box: top of the Outlook window. from:address finds mail from that sender.
  • Search for a few words from a long subject, not always the whole line.

Lesson 6. Lesson 6 — Junk and phishing

  • Junk is often unwanted advertising. Outlook may move it to Junk Email.
  • Phishing tries to trick you into clicking, opening a file, or giving away a password or other secrets.
  • Pause. Do not click. Do not open an unexpected attachment. Do not reply with a password, PIN, or code.
  • Check the sender’s address, spelling, urgency, and odd requests (Get Cyber Safe).
  • Verify through official details you find yourself, not details inside the message.
  • Tell staff at work. Report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501.
  • If you were a victim, also contact local police. More practice: Email & Online Safety Basics in Canada.

Lesson 7. Lesson 7 — Appointments and meetings

  • Appointment = your own time, no guests, no room. New Appointment or Ctrl+Shift+A.
  • Meeting = other people, and it can include a room. New Meeting or Ctrl+Shift+Q.
  • Give every item a clear title, date, start time, and end time.
  • Set a reminder from the Reminder list, or choose None.
  • Save an appointment. Send a meeting request.
  • Meeting replies arrive in your Inbox.
  • Use a practice account. Do not invite real people unless your tutor agrees.

Lesson 8. Lesson 8 — Contacts, tasks, and habits

  • People > New Contact (Ctrl+Shift+C). A name and email are enough.
  • Do not store passwords, SINs, or other private numbers in a contact.
  • New Items > Task, then Save & Close (Ctrl+Shift+K).
  • A flag and a task can track the same follow-up — use the list you will actually open.
  • Daily habits: read Mail, reply with care, file or flag, check Calendar.
  • Pause on suspicious mail. Sign out on a shared computer.
