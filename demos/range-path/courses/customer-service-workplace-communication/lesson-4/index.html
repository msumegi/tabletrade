Lesson 4 of 5

Handle a difficult interaction with care

Not every customer or coworker will be calm. Skills for Success collaboration includes managing difficult interactions with other people: having productive discussions, addressing interpersonal barriers, and discussing, negotiating, and resolving those interactions in a sensitive and helpful manner.

Communication skills support that work. Listen first. Ask questions to confirm what the person needs. Detect their purpose — are they asking for a refund of time, a correction, or simply to be heard? Then speak with purpose: name the problem in plain words, say what you can do, and say what you cannot do.

Stay respectful even if the other person is frustrated. Collaboration also asks you to engage in trust-building behaviour and to use cooperative language. You do not have to accept abuse. If you need help, that is another moment for collaboration: ask a coworker or a lead to join the conversation.

A simple pattern you can practise:

  1. Listen without interrupting.
  2. Confirm: “I hear that you waited and the door was locked. Is that right?”
  3. Offer the next step you can actually take: “I will check with the coordinator and call you back today.”
  4. If you and the person cannot agree, stay calm and ask for help rather than inventing a promise.

This pattern is for in-person or phone conversations. It is not a script for writing complaint emails, and it is not legal advice about refusing service. If safety is at risk, follow your workplace’s safety process.
