Lesson 2 of 5

Speak clearly and adapt to the person in front of you

After you understand the request, you need to answer in a way the other person can follow. Skills for Success communication includes speaking with clarity and speaking with purpose.

Speaking with clarity means using grammar, pronunciation (even with an accent), and a pace that fit the situation. You do not need a “perfect” accent. You do need the other person to be able to follow you.

Speaking with purpose means choosing examples, facts, content, and structure that match your goal — for example, to convey or summarize information, to explain something, or to persuade. The listener should understand the purpose of your message.

You also need to adapt to your audience and context. Identify the needs, preferences, and interests of the people you are talking with, including different communication styles that may come from culture or ability. Then choose content, tone, language, gesture, and approach that fit that audience and that setting.

A short, plain answer works well when someone asks, “What time does the supper start?” A slower explanation, with one idea at a time, works better when someone is upset or is hearing the information for the first time. If you do not know the answer, say so clearly and offer the next step: “I will ask my coworker and come back to you.”

Do not hide behind jargon. If you must use a workplace word, explain it. This is not a lesson in writing workplace email, and it is not a spelling drill. It is spoken service: clear words, a clear purpose, and a tone that fits the person in front of you.
