Drive to conditions

Posted speed limits assume ideal roads. Alberta.ca states you must drive according to conditions — and you can get a ticket if you do not. Slow down on ice, snow, and in poor visibility.

Following distance: On slick roads, Alberta.ca advises allowing at least three times the normal following distance. AMA rural advice suggests thinking in seconds — for example doubling a three-second gap toward about six seconds when roads are treacherous.

Smooth inputs: Avoid sudden steering or braking that can cause a skid. Do not use cruise control in winter conditions (Alberta.ca).

Lights: Use headlights so others see your taillights — do not rely only on daytime running lights. In snow or fog, use low beams; high beams can create glare (AMA and general winter guidance).

Snowplows: Stay back until they let you pass. Do not drive into the snow cloud. Older Alberta travel tips also stress patience on two-lane highways.

Bridges and hills: Bridge decks often get slick sooner. Gear choices and gentle braking matter — learn your vehicle with a qualified instructor if you are a new driver; this course is not behind-the-wheel training.
