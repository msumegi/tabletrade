Keyboarding is a digital skill

Skills for Success are skills that help you in work, learning, and life. The Government of Canada says they help you get a job, progress at your current job, change jobs, take part in your community, and succeed in learning. This course focuses on one of those skills: Digital.

Digital skill is your ability to use digital technology and tools to find, manage, apply, create, and share information and content. The official page says most jobs now use digital skills, and you also need them when you apply other skills such as reading, writing, and numeracy.

One Digital component is to use digital devices, including computers, tablets, smartphones, and other handheld devices. That includes identifying the goal of a digital task, using basic functions that are common to most devices, and knowing basic terms. Typing on a keyboard — on a laptop, a plugged-in keyboard, or a tablet — is one of those basic functions. You use it to write a notice, fill in a form, or search for information.

Another Digital component is to use common digital tools to complete tasks. The Government of Canada page lists examples such as word-processing or spreadsheet software. This course does not tell you which brand to buy. The same component says to keep digital tools up to date, for example by downloading updates, and to use digital tools that enhance accessibility when you need them, such as a screen magnifier or other assistive technologies.

You do not need a new computer to start. Practise with the device you already have. This course is not a speed test and it does not promise a job.
