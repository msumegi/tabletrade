Lesson 4 of 5

Employment standards pointers, not HR advice

Health and safety rules are not the only official rules at work. Employment standards set out rights and obligations of employers and employees in Alberta, including minimum standards employers must provide. The Alberta Employment Standards page is the starting map. It points to rules about minimum wage, overtime, holidays, leaves, vacations, hours of work, earnings, youth workers, and termination. This course does not teach those topics in full, and it does not tell you what to pay someone.

One page many new supervisors look up is “Hours of work and rest.” As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), that page said:

  • An employee’s work must stay within 12 consecutive hours a day, unless an exception applies (for example, an accident, urgent work, or a variance).
  • For a shift of more than 5 hours but less than 10 hours, the employee is entitled to at least one 30-minute break.
  • For a shift of 10 hours or more, the employee is entitled to two 30-minute breaks.
  • A shift of 5 hours or less has no break entitlement on that page.
  • Employees are entitled to at least one day of rest each work week.

The same page lists exceptions and exempt occupations, including managers, supervisors, and people employed in a confidential capacity. If your role is called “supervisor,” do not guess which rules apply. Read the live page. If the page differs from the legislation, Alberta says the legislation is considered correct.

To ask a question, use the official contact page. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Employment Standards listed 780-427-3731 in the Edmonton area and toll free 1-877-427-3731 in Alberta. Online questions receive a response within 3 business days. That form cannot be used to make a complaint.
