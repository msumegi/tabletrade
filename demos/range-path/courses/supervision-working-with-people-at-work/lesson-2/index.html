Supervisor and worker health-and-safety duties

Everyone on a work site has duties. This lesson names the ones Alberta lists for supervisors and workers. It does not replace the official guide or the law.

Supervisors are responsible for:

  • protecting the health and safety of the workers they supervise
  • making sure those workers work in a healthy and safe manner, follow OHS legislation, and are not subject to or participating in harassment and violence
  • advising their workers of all known or reasonably foreseeable work hazards
  • reporting health and safety concerns and conditions to their employer
  • cooperating with any person who is exercising a duty under the OHS legislation

If you supervise people, a plain way to remember this is: tell people about hazards you know or can reasonably foresee, help them work safely, do not ignore harassment or violence, and tell your employer when something is wrong.

Workers also have duties. They must protect their own health and safety and that of others at or near the work site. They must cooperate with their employer or supervisor by following the health and safety rules for the job. They must use hazard controls and wear the personal protective equipment the employer designates or the law requires. They must not cause or take part in harassment and violence. They must report health and safety concerns to their employer or supervisor, take part in training the employer provides, and cooperate with anyone exercising an OHS duty.

ALIS, a Government of Alberta site, adds that supervisors should take action to protect workers, including from violence and harassment, make sure people can do the work safely, and report concerns to the right people.

These duties go both ways. A supervisor who only “gives orders” and never listens to a safety concern is not meeting the official picture.
