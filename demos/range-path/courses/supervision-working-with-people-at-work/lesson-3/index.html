Workers’ rights and refusing dangerous work

ALIS says workers have three fundamental health and safety rights under Alberta’s Occupational Health and Safety Act:

  1. The right to refuse unsafe work
  2. The right to know about workplace hazards
  3. The right to participate in health and safety decisions

The right to know means workers should be told about hazards and about what is being done to control or eliminate them. The right to participate means workers can take part in decisions that affect their health and safety. Many workplaces have a health and safety committee or a representative who speaks for workers.

The official OHS page “Refuse dangerous work” explains the right to refuse in more detail. Workers on provincially regulated work sites can refuse work if they reasonably think there is an undue hazard. An undue hazard is a serious and immediate threat to health and safety that the refusing worker actually observes or experiences at the work site.

Workers are protected from reprisal for exercising this right. Employers cannot take or threaten disciplinary action against a worker for exercising their rights and duties under the legislation.

If you refuse work you think is dangerous:

  1. Do not do that work.
  2. Tell your employer, supervisor, or another designated person as soon as you can what you are refusing and why.
  3. As much as possible, make sure your refusal does not endanger others.
  4. Your employer must look into the hazard and take action.
  5. You can do other work your employer assigns if you can reasonably do it and it is safe.

If your employer will not stop work you think is dangerous, contact the OHS Contact Centre (1-866-415-8690 as of 10 November 2026, America/Regina). This course cannot decide a real refusal for you.
