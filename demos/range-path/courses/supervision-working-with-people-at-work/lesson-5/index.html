Skills for working with people, including leading a small group

Safety rules and employment standards tell you what the law expects. Day-to-day work also depends on how people treat one another. Canada’s Skills for Success describe skills that help you at work, in learning, and in life. Two skills matter a great deal when you supervise or work beside others: collaboration and communication.

Collaboration is your ability to contribute and support others to achieve a common goal. The official components include:

  • working well with other people, such as being on time and encouraging cooperative behaviour
  • valuing diversity and inclusivity, and responding without judging people for different opinions
  • managing difficult interactions in a sensitive and helpful way
  • acknowledging the roles of yourself and others
  • supporting others through coaching, mentoring, and motivating
  • taking responsibility for your own tasks, sharing information, and making sure others have a chance to contribute
  • reflecting on how the team did and using feedback in a constructive way

Skills for Success does not list a separate skill called “leadership.” Supporting others through coaching, mentoring, and motivating is part of collaboration. That is the official leadership pointer in this course.

Communication is your ability to receive, understand, consider, and share information by speaking, listening, and interacting. Useful parts include listening with attention, asking questions to confirm what you heard, speaking clearly and with a purpose, and adapting your words to the people you are talking with.

These skills help a lead hand or new supervisor work with people. They do not replace OHS or employment standards, and they do not make you an HR manager. Practise them at work, and check official pages when a safety or pay question comes up.
