What “supervisor” means on an Alberta work site

This course is for people who lead a small team, fill in as a lead hand, or want to work better with others at work. It is not an HR course. It does not teach you how to hire, fire, or discipline someone. It is not legal advice.

Alberta’s occupational health and safety (OHS) law uses the idea of an internal responsibility system. The Government of Alberta says every person on a work site is responsible for working toward a healthy and safe workplace, and every work site party is accountable for workplace health and safety.

In OHS language, a supervisor is a person who is in charge of a work site or has authority over one or more workers. The title on a name badge is not what decides this. If you tell other people what to do, or you are in charge of a site, Alberta’s supervisor duties can apply to you.

An employer includes a person who employs or engages one or more workers. Employers must make sure workers are supervised by supervisors who are competent and familiar with the OHS rules that apply to the work. They must also make workers aware of their rights and responsibilities under OHS legislation.

A worker is any person engaged in an occupation. That can include a volunteer who performs or supplies services for an organization or employer. Some people, such as unpaid students in a learning activity, are not considered workers on that page.

If you are unsure who the supervisor is on your site, ask your employer. If something feels unsafe, you can also contact the OHS Contact Centre. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta listed 780-415-8690 in Edmonton and toll free 1-866-415-8690.
