Name the common business structures

Before you register anything, it helps to know the common business structures in Canada. The CRA says the structure you choose has a significant effect on the way you report your income, and on many other matters. This lesson names the structures. It does not tell you which one to pick.

A sole proprietorship is an unincorporated business owned by one person. The CRA calls it the simplest kind of business structure. The owner makes the decisions, receives the profits, claims the losses, and does not have separate legal status from the business. The owner also assumes the risks of the business. Those risks can reach personal property and assets. You may register a business name, operate under your own name, or both.

A partnership, on the CRA page, is an association or relationship between two or more individuals, corporations, trusts, or partnerships that join together to carry on a trade or business. Partners contribute money, labour, property, or skills. In return, each partner is entitled to a share of the profits or losses. The Government of Alberta says partners share the profits and the risks or debts of the business.

A corporation is a separate legal entity. It can enter into contracts and own property in its own name. Alberta’s incorporation page says a corporation acts as a “person.” It can buy, sell, and own assets, including land, make contracts, sue, and be sued. You do not need to be a large business to register as a corporation. A small business with only one owner can do so.

Alberta’s registry pages say that if you need help deciding whether a sole proprietorship, partnership, or corporation is the best form, you should get legal advice. That is the right next step. This course will not choose for you.
