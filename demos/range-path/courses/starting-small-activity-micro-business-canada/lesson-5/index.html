Check licences and permits before you operate

A business licence is not the same as a registered business name or a business number. You may need none, one, or several licences, depending on what you do and where you do it.

Alberta’s Consumer Programs page says it licenses some businesses and individuals, and registers charitable organizations, under laws such as the Consumer Protection Act. It is an offence to operate without the required licence or charitable registration. Other businesses may be licensed through other government departments, municipalities, or delegated agencies. Some businesses do not need any type of licence.

If you are not sure, contact the business licensing office. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Alberta listed 780-422-1335, toll free 310-0000 before the number in Alberta, and sartr.businesslicensing@gov.ab.ca.

A licence is not an endorsement by the Government of Alberta, and it does not guarantee the quality of goods or services.

You may also need permits and licences from federal, provincial or territorial, and municipal governments. Canada.ca points to BizPaL. Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada says BizPaL builds a personalized list of the documents you may need from those levels of government. Start from the official BizPaL page and follow the link to bizpal.ca.

This course cannot tell you which licence your activity needs. Use the official pages, ask the licensing office, and check your municipality. Do not treat a completed course as permission to operate.
