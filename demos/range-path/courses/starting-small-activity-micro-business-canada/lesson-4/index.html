Find Alberta registries and register the right thing

In Alberta, registering a business name and incorporating a corporation are different steps. Both are done through authorized service providers.

A business name, Alberta says, does not have a legal existence of its own. It is a name used to represent the business to the public. The sole proprietor or partners remain personally responsible for the debts and obligations of the business. Registering a name does not give you ownership of that name. It is proof that a particular business is using it.

A trade name (also called a sole proprietorship on that page) is used when an individual does business under a name other than their personal name. To register, you choose a name, may get an optional Business Name Report, complete a Declaration of Trade Name, and take your information to an authorized Corporate Registry service provider with valid ID and fee payment. You cannot put words such as “Limited,” “Incorporated,” or “Corporation” — or Ltd., Inc., or Corp. — at the end of a business name, because that would suggest you are running a corporation.

To incorporate an Alberta corporation you follow the longer checklist on Alberta’s incorporation page. That checklist includes a name with a legal element (such as Ltd. or Inc.), a NUANS report for most word names, required notices, at least one adult director, and an agent for service in Alberta. If your information meets the requirements, the service provider gives you a certificate of incorporation.

Find providers on Alberta’s “Find a business registry” page. Registry agents charge a service fee that is not regulated and may vary. There is also a government fee for certain registrations. This course does not quote those fees.

As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), Corporate Registry listed 780-427-7013, and 310-0000 before the number in Alberta.
