Why French can help in daily life

IRCC says English or French language skills are very important to help you settle in Canada. You may choose to focus on learning or improving one or the other. That often depends on which of the two languages most people speak where you live.

Strong English or French skills will help with:

  • getting a job
  • going to school
  • accessing services
  • helping your children with school work
  • meeting and talking to people
  • getting your Canadian citizenship

If you already speak one official language, IRCC says you should think about learning the other. In many parts of Canada, being able to speak both is an advantage for finding a job and taking part in your community.

Even in a community where English is more common, a little French can help you greet a francophone neighbour, read the other side of a bilingual federal page, or support a child who is learning French in school. Canadian Heritage’s work with provinces and territories includes helping official-language minority communities receive services in their own language, and helping young Canadians learn French and English as a second language.

Language skills for some regulated jobs and trades may need to be stronger than the skills used to immigrate. Those jobs may require fluency and knowledge of work-related language, including phrases unique to Canada. This course does not meet those job-language requirements, and it does not replace a test or a professional language class.

Start where you are. A few minutes of noticing French around you is a real step, even if you are not ready for a full course.
