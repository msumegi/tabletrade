Lesson 5 of 5

Keep practising, without a fluency promise

IRCC encourages people to take steps to improve French or English as soon as they can. You do not have to wait until you feel “ready.” A small, regular habit is more useful than waiting for perfect study time.

Try this weekly practice:

  1. Open one federal web page you already use and switch it to Français. Write down three words you recognized and three you did not.
  2. Say those new words out loud once. You are practising sound as well as sight.
  3. If you have a child in school, ask what French words they are learning and practise one of them together. IRCC lists helping children with school work as a reason language skills matter.
  4. If you want a class, ask a local newcomer organization or your adult learning council how to find a language assessment centre.

Canadian Heritage promotes French and English in Canadian society and encourages activities that foster mutual understanding between Anglophones and Francophones. Your practice is part of that larger picture, even if your first sentences are short.

Be kind to yourself. This course is an introduction to French in daily Canadian life. It is not a promise that you will become fluent, get a job, or pass a language test. Fluency, when someone reaches it, comes from much more practice than five short lessons.

If a word on an official page is hard, look at the English version of the same page. Using both languages side by side is one advantage of bilingual federal services.
