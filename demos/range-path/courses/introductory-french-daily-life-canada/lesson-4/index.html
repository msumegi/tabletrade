Official places to start learning more

IRCC points to the Language Portal of Canada as a place with a variety of tools and resources to improve your language skills. This course does not replace that portal, and it does not copy its quizzes. If you want official writing and learning tools, start from the IRCC page and follow its link, or search for the Language Portal of Canada on Canada.ca.

You can also take language classes to learn English or French once you are in Canada. IRCC says federal, provincial, and territorial governments across Canada fund language classes. You will need to pay for private language classes. To find provincial or territorial programs, ask an organization that helps newcomers in your area, or visit the newcomers website for the province or territory where you live.

Before you start a funded class, IRCC says you must get tested to find out your current language skills. Visit a language assessment centre and say you are interested in taking language classes. This Range Path course is not that assessment, and finishing it does not place you in a funded class.

There are official tests that measure French, such as the Test d’évaluation de français (TEF) and the Diplôme d’études en langue française (DELF). People sometimes need a test result for a job or for college. This course does not prepare you for those tests and does not give you a certificate you can use in their place.

Canadian Heritage’s Official Languages pages also collect learning and funding information at a government level. Use those pages to see what the government supports. Do not treat a volunteer conversation group, an app, or this course as an official language credential.
