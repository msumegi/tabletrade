Lesson 3 of 5

Notice French in the Canada around you

You do not have to wait for a classroom to meet French. Because federal services and publications are offered in both official languages, many pages you already use have a French version. Look for a language button that says Français. Open a page you know well — for example a weather, benefits, or parks page — and switch it to French. See which words you recognize.

You will also see French beside English on many federal forms and signs. That pairing is part of how the government offers services in both languages. Try reading the French line after you read the English one. You are training your eye, not taking a test.

Canadian Heritage says the bilingual character of the country is a fundamental part of national identity. It emphasizes the advantages of that bilingual character, helps voluntary organizations reflect it in their own activities, and encourages activities that foster mutual understanding between Anglophones and Francophones.

Heritage also supports official-language minority communities so they can take part in the economic, cultural, and social life of the country. In practical terms, that means French is not only “a Quebec language.” Francophone communities exist across Canada, including in Alberta.

Listen for French at a community event, on a radio station, or when a neighbour greets you. You do not need to answer in full sentences yet. Noticing the language is the habit this lesson asks for.

This course does not invent a list of phrases. Learn words from official bilingual pages you already trust, rather than from a random website.
