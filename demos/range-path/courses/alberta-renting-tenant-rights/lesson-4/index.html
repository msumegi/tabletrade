Lesson 4 of 5

Ending a tenancy and getting a deposit back

How a tenancy ends depends on the type of agreement. A fixed-term tenancy ends on the date in the agreement. Alberta.ca says neither side needs to give notice to end it on that date (though telling each other early is described as a good practice, not a legal requirement). Unless you both agree otherwise, the tenant must move out by noon on that end date.

To end a periodic tenancy, written notice is required. The notice must include the address, the date the tenancy will end, and the signature of the person giving notice. Landlords must also include their reasons. The amount of notice depends on who is giving it and whether the tenancy is weekly, monthly, or yearly. As of November 2026, the page said a tenant ending a month-to-month tenancy must give one full tenancy month of notice, and a landlord must give three full tenancy months. Weekly and yearly tenancies have different times. Always check the live “Ending a tenancy” page. Those times can change, and other rules apply to evictions and “substantial breach.”

Alberta.ca also says tenants cannot withhold rent to force a landlord to do something such as making repairs. The landlord is entitled to have the rent paid in full when it is due.

If there are no deductions, the landlord must return the full deposit plus interest within 10 days after the tenant gave up possession. If there are deductions, the page describes other 10-day and 30-day steps. Date-stamp: November 2026. Provide a forwarding address in writing and keep copies of your papers for 3 years.

This lesson does not draft your notice or tell you whether an eviction notice you received is valid.
