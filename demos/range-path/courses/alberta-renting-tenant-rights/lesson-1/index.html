What this course is, and what the RTA is

If you rent the place where you live in Alberta, a provincial law called the Residential Tenancies Act (RTA) sets minimum standards for landlords and tenants. The official Alberta.ca page on rights and responsibilities says the RTA is a framework for how the two sides must treat each other.

When a landlord and tenant agree to rent a home, they enter a residential tenancy agreement. Alberta.ca says this agreement can be written, oral, or implied, but it should be written so both sides have a record. A later page on starting a tenancy says an agreement cannot take away rights set out in the RTA. Anything in an agreement that tries to waive those rights is void.

This course is a map of official pages. It does not tell you what to do in your dispute. It does not write your notice or fill in an RTDRS form. The Centre for Public Legal Education Alberta (CPLEA) also publishes Renting 101 and related pages. CPLEA says that guide provides general legal information on Alberta law only. Use it as extra reading, not as a replacement for Alberta.ca or for advice about your own case.

If you rent only the land under a mobile home, a different law (the Mobile Home Sites Tenancies Act) may apply. Read the official page for that situation.

For questions, Alberta’s Consumer Contact Centre is listed on the official contact page (see Lesson 5).
