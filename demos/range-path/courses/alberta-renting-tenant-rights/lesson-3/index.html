Lesson 3 of 5

Agreements, deposits, and inspection reports

Alberta has two main kinds of rental agreements. A fixed-term tenancy begins and ends on specific dates. A periodic tenancy has a start date but no end date (often month-to-month). Either side may end a periodic tenancy by giving notice, following the official rules.

Landlords usually ask for a security deposit (sometimes called a damage deposit). As of November 2026, Alberta.ca said the deposit cannot be more than one month’s rent at the time the tenancy starts. It cannot be increased later if the rent goes up. Landlords must put the deposit into an interest-bearing trust account in Alberta within 2 banking days of collecting it. Tenants can ask for a receipt. Check the live page; these details can change.

When the tenancy ends, tenants have the right to get the deposit back, with any interest owing, if there is no damage beyond normal wear and tear, the place has been properly cleaned if the agreement requires it, and no rent or other costs are owing. Normal wear and tear means deterioration that happens over time with use, even with reasonable care.

Move-in and move-out inspection reports are mandatory. They describe the condition of the home when you move in and when you move out. You can use them to show you did not cause damage that was already there. If the inspection-report rules are not met, the landlord cannot deduct damages or cleaning costs from the deposit (the landlord may still try to recover costs another way). Keep copies.

This course does not calculate your deposit or complete your inspection form.
