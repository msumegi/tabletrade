Getting help and the RTDRS

If you have a problem, Alberta.ca says to try to resolve it before filing a complaint or applying for a formal remedy. Talk calmly, write down what was said, and keep copies of letters or emails. State facts. Do not include threats.

If you believe a landlord has committed an RTA offence, you can contact the Service Alberta / Consumer Contact Centre to ask about an investigation. As of November 2026, the contact page listed: 780-427-4088, toll-free 1-877-427-4088, and rta@gov.ab.ca, weekdays 8:15 am to 4:30 pm (closed statutory holidays).

If the issue is still not resolved, a tenant or a landlord may apply to the Residential Tenancy Dispute Resolution Service (RTDRS), or they may go to court. RTDRS can deal with concerns such as ending a tenancy, unpaid rent or utilities, security deposits, damages, repairs, and other common disagreements. As of November 2026, the overview said you can apply within 2 years from the date the claim or loss first came to your attention, if the claim is under $100,000. Hearings are by telephone. You must file an application, serve the other party, take part in the hearing, and enforce any order. This course cannot predict the result and does not file the application for you.

RTDRS contact listed on that page: 780-644-3000 and rtdrs@gov.ab.ca. The page says RTDRS does not accept applications or evidence at that email address.

CPLEA’s renting pages are extra general information. For a live dispute, get help from official contacts or a legal clinic.
