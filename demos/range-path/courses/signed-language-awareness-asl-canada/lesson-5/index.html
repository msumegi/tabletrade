Lesson 5 of 5

Where to learn more, and what this course is not

If signed languages matter in your work or community, keep learning from official and Deaf-led sources.

Useful starting points:

  • The Accessible Canada Act text and the Government of Canada summary, for the legal recognition of ASL, LSQ, and Indigenous sign languages
  • CAD-ASC (cad-asc.ca), for national Deaf-community positions on language and interpreting
  • Canada VRS (srvcanadavrs.ca), if you or someone you support is a primary ASL or LSQ user who needs to make a voice telephone call
  • Local Deaf organizations and classes taught by qualified Deaf teachers. CAD-ASC says qualified Deaf teachers should teach Sign language

Government of Canada guidance on consulting persons with disabilities uses the principle “Nothing without us.” That means Deaf people should be involved when programs and services are designed, not only talked about after decisions are made.

Do not:

  • Pretend this five-lesson awareness course is interpreter training
  • Use a family member as a professional interpreter for important or private conversations
  • Invent signs and call them ASL or LSQ
  • Assume one signed language works for every Deaf person in Canada

A respectful next step is simple: learn whose language is being used, arrange proper access when it is needed, and keep studying with people who are qualified to teach.
