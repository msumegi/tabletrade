Canada’s Video Relay Service

Canada has a national Video Relay Service, often called Canada VRS. It is administered by the Canadian Administrator of Video Relay Service (CAV), a not-for-profit organization mandated by the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC).

Canada VRS’s own pages say the service lets Deaf, DeafBlind, hard-of-hearing, and other Canadians who use ASL or LSQ as their primary language communicate with voice telephone users through internet video. A sign-language interpreter relays the conversation in real time between signed language and spoken language.

The service is free to use. To register, you must be a primary sign language user. After you register, you receive a personal VRS number so you can make and receive calls. CAV says Canada VRS launched on September 28, 2016, and is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. Apps are described for iOS, Android, Windows, and Mac computers.

Government of Canada accessibility guidance describes VRS in similar terms: a person using sign language can make telephone and videoconference calls through an operator who interprets their signing verbally, and the other way around.

VRS is a telephone-access tool. It is not the same as booking an in-person interpreter for a long meeting, and this course does not register you for the service. Hearing colleagues who are not primary sign language users should not treat the VRS app as their own interpreter training.

For help, Canada VRS lists support@srvcanadavrs.ca and 1-800-958-5856 (English and French). Always check the live page, because contact details can change.
