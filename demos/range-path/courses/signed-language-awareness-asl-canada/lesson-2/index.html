Lesson 2 of 5

ASL and LSQ are real languages

The Canadian Association of the Deaf – Association des Sourds du Canada (CAD-ASC) is a national Deaf organization. On its language position page it says the natural language of Deaf people is Sign language, and that in Canada there are two legitimate Sign languages: American Sign Language (ASL) and la Langue des Signes Quebecoise (LSQ). It also notes a regional dialect called Maritimes Sign Language (MSL).

CAD-ASC describes these Sign languages as real languages with their own grammar, syntax, and vocabulary. They are not “English on the hands” or “French on the hands.” A word-for-word signed copy of an English sentence is not the same thing as ASL.

On its official-languages page, CAD-ASC says it uses ASL and LSQ as its two official languages, with English and French as secondary languages. It also says captioning, lip-reading, assistive devices, and oral interpretation are other ways some people access language, but those modes are not the same as ASL or LSQ.

You cannot assume every Deaf person lip-reads, speaks, or uses the same signed language. A person in much of English-speaking Canada may use ASL. A person in Quebec may use LSQ. Some people use more than one language. Some people are Deafblind and may use other tactile or visual ways to communicate.

This lesson is awareness, not a grammar class. Do not invent signs from English word order and call it ASL.
