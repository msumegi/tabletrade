What this course is, and which languages the law names

Signed languages are languages. People use their hands, face, and body to share meaning. This course explains that idea in a Canadian context. It does not teach you enough ASL to interpret a meeting, a medical appointment, or a legal matter.

The Accessible Canada Act is a federal law. The Department of Justice text of section 5.1 says American Sign Language, Quebec Sign Language, and Indigenous sign languages are recognized as the primary languages for communication by deaf persons in Canada. A Government of Canada summary of the Act uses the French name Langue des signes québécoise (LSQ) for Quebec Sign Language. That summary also says the purpose of the Act is to make Canada barrier-free by January 1, 2040, in areas that include communication.

“Primary language” here means these signed languages are recognized in that law as the main languages Deaf people use to communicate in Canada. It does not mean every Deaf person uses the same language, and it does not make you an interpreter.

The same Government of Canada summary notes that Canada already has a human-rights framework that includes the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, the Canadian Human Rights Act, and Canada’s commitments under the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. This course does not interpret those laws for your situation.

If you want to learn to sign, look for classes taught by qualified Deaf teachers after you finish these awareness lessons.
