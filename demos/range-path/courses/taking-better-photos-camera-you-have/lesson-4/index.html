Lesson 4 of 5

Use natural light when you can

Parks Canada’s fifth tip is to use natural light. Early morning and late afternoon light — called the golden hours on that page — add warmth and depth to your photos. Avoid harsh midday sun when you can.

You do not control the sun, but you can choose when and where you stand. If a face looks harshly lit at noon, turn the person so the sun is not straight in their eyes, or wait for a bit of shade, or try again later in the day. If you are photographing a building or a field, the same scene often looks softer near the beginning or end of the day.

Parks Canada’s video says to use natural light conditions to your advantage. That includes noticing what the light is doing: is it coming from the side, from behind you, or from behind your subject? Side light can show shape. Light straight from above at midday can make strong, unflattering shadows.

You do not need extra lights or a studio for this course. A window on a cloudy day can be gentle indoor light for a photo of an object on a table. Step nearer to the window rather than turning on a mixed set of lamps if the colours look odd — and if that still does not work, try again at a different time.

Practise by photographing the same outdoor subject twice: once near the middle of the day and once in the early morning or late afternoon. Compare which version feels warmer and easier to look at.
