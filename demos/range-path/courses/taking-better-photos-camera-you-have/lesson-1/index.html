Lesson 1 of 5

Decide what the photo is about

You do not need a new camera to take a better photo. Parks Canada says you can capture strong wildlife photos from a safe distance with gear such as binoculars or a telephoto lens, or even your phone. This course uses the camera you already have, including a phone. It does not tell you which brand to buy.

Parks Canada’s first tip is: identify your subject. Focus on what you want to highlight — a mountain peak, a bird, an historic site, or something closer to home, such as a person at a community table, a handmade sign, or a garden — and make it the star of the shot.

Before you press the button, pause and ask: “What is this photo about?” If you cannot answer in one short sentence, the picture may have too many things competing for attention.

Move closer only when it is safe and respectful to do so, or crop with your feet by taking a step to the side so the main thing fills more of the frame. Parks Canada’s video about these tips says to identify your subject and make it stand out.

Practise with three photos of the same scene. In the first, include everything around you. In the second, choose one subject and make it clearer. In the third, try again. Compare them on your screen. The “better” photo is often the one where a viewer can tell right away what you wanted them to see.

This habit works at a national park, at the hall, or in your yard.
