Lesson 5 of 5

Stay safe and follow the rules

A good photo is never worth harm to you, to wildlife, or to a protected place. Parks Canada asks you to capture moments safely, respectfully, and sustainably.

When you photograph animals, give them space. Parks Canada says that being discreet and respectful helps wildlife survive and keeps you safer. As of 10 November 2026 (America/Regina), the page said:

  • Never take photos of people or selfies with wildlife in the background.
  • Stay at least 30 metres (about 3 bus lengths) from large animals such as deer, moose, and elk.
  • Stay at least 100 metres (about 10 bus lengths) from bears, wolves, coyotes, and cougars.
  • Do not approach wildlife or dens, and do not let wildlife approach you. If your presence causes them to move, you are too close.

You can still take the photo from that safe distance, including with your phone.

Drones are prohibited in all Parks Canada locations. They can disturb wildlife and create a risk of injury.

Each Parks Canada location may have its own photography guidelines. Commercial filming and photography need business licensing and permits. Before you visit, check that destination’s website and follow the rules that protect the place.

These safety ideas also travel: do not startle farm animals, and do not step into traffic for a picture. This course is not a permit to film for money, and it is not a brand advertisement. Use the camera you have, keep people and animals safe, and enjoy the practice.
