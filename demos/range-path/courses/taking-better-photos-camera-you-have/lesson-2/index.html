Use the rule of thirds

Parks Canada’s second tip is to use the rule of thirds. Divide your image into thirds to create a balanced composition.

Imagine two lines across the picture and two lines down it, like a tic-tac-toe board. You now have nine smaller rectangles and four places where the lines cross. Parks Canada’s photo-tips video says to compose with the rule of thirds and on their intersections. That means the important thing in the photo does not always have to sit dead-centre. It can sit along a line, or where two lines meet.

On many phones you can turn on a grid in the camera settings so you can see those lines while you shoot. If your camera has no grid, imagine the lines before you take the picture.

Try this: photograph a person, a doorway, or a tree. First put the subject in the exact middle. Then move the camera so the subject sits on a left or right third, or on an intersection. Compare the two photos. Neither is “wrong,” but the off-centre version often feels more balanced, which is what Parks Canada is pointing to.

The rule of thirds is a guide, not a law. If a circular fountain or a face looks best in the centre, you may keep it there. The skill is to choose on purpose, not by accident.

Practise ten photos this week using the grid. You are training your eye, not buying new equipment.
