Lesson 3 of 5

Look for symmetry and try a new angle

Parks Canada’s third tip is to look for natural symmetry. Nature is full of it — reflections in lakes, rows of trees, or mountain ridges. You can use those shapes to make a stronger picture.

You can practise the same idea close to home. A puddle after rain, a window reflection, a row of chairs, or two matching doorways can give you that balanced, repeated shape. Stand still until the reflection or the row is clear, then take the photo.

The fourth tip is to try new perspectives. Change your angle. Parks Canada suggests shooting from ground level or looking through natural frames such as branches or arches. The video shows pictures taken close to the ground or close to the water.

You do not need a special lens to change your point of view. Bend your knees. Hold the phone lower. Step to one side. Look through a doorway or a window so the opening frames your subject. Then take the same scene from standing height and compare.

Be safe while you do this. Do not lie in a roadway, lean over a drop, or step onto ice to “get the shot.” A new angle should still be a safe place to stand.

Take two versions of one subject today: one from your usual standing height, and one from a clearly different angle. Keep the one where the subject is easier to read.
