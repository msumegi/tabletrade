Meet your everyday devices

A digital device is a tool that stores and shares information. Canada’s Skills for Success program calls digital skill the ability to use digital technology and tools to find, manage, apply, create, and share information and content. That includes computers, tablets, smartphones, and other handheld devices.

Common devices:

  • A desktop computer usually stays on a desk and uses a separate screen, keyboard, and mouse.
  • A laptop is a portable computer with a built-in screen and keyboard.
  • A tablet is a flat screen you control mostly by touch.
  • A smartphone is a phone that also works like a small computer. Get Cyber Safe notes that people use phones as cameras, maps, and more — not only to make calls.

You do not need to own every device. Many libraries and adult learning centres have computers you can practise on.

Shared ideas on most devices:

  • A screen shows what is happening.
  • You give commands by tapping, clicking, or typing.
  • Settings (sometimes a gear icon) let you change things such as volume, brightness, or Wi-Fi.
  • Apps or programs are tools you open to do a job, such as mail, maps, or a web browser.

Skills for Success also asks learners to know basic words that most devices share, and to use basic functions that are common to most of them. If a word is new, write it down and ask a tutor. Learning the names helps you follow instructions later.

This course will not repair a broken device. If something will not turn on, check the power or charger first, then ask for help.
