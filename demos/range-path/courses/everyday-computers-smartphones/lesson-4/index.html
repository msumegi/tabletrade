Connect to the internet with care

The internet lets your device visit websites, send messages, and use many apps. A common way to connect is Wi-Fi.

On Windows 11, Microsoft’s steps are: select the Network, Sound, or Battery icons on the taskbar to open quick settings; on the Wi-Fi setting, choose Manage Wi-Fi connections; pick the network you want; select Connect; then type the network password and select Next. On Windows 10, select the Network icon on the taskbar, choose the network, select Connect, and type the password.

Use a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi or a network a staff person names at the learning centre. Get Cyber Safe warns that public Wi-Fi is convenient but less safe. Anyone can join, including people who should not see your information. Criminals sometimes set up fake hotspots with a name that looks almost real. When you can, ask someone at the place hosting the Wi-Fi for the real network name.

If you must use public Wi-Fi:

  • Do not use it for banking or other sensitive information, or to make a purchase.
  • Turn Wi-Fi off when you do not need it.
  • Prefer websites that show a padlock in the address area (often a sign of a more secure HTTPS connection).
  • Watch for people reading your screen over your shoulder.

Get Cyber Safe also says that if you use public Wi-Fi often, a VPN (virtual private network) can be a safer option because it encrypts your data. Ask a tutor before you buy or install one. This course does not recommend a brand.
