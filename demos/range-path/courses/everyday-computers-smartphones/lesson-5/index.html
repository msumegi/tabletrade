Simple habits that keep devices safer

You do not need to become a security expert. A few regular habits help a lot.

1. Update your software. Get Cyber Safe says updates often add security protections, not only new features. Skipping updates can leave a device more open to malware and hacking. Turn on automatic updates when you can, for the operating system, apps, browsers, and security software. On a phone, update when the device asks, or turn on automatic updates.

2. Use strong, unique sign-ins. Get Cyber Safe recommends a passphrase of four or more random words and at least 15 characters when you can. If you use a shorter password style, use at least twelve characters with a mix of letter cases, a number, and a symbol. Use a different password for each important account. Never share a password. Do not send a password by email or text.

3. Download with care. On a computer, scan downloads with security software and delete anything it flags. On a phone, Get Cyber Safe says to install apps from trusted stores such as the App Store or Google Play, and to read the permissions an app asks for.

4. Think before you tap. Unexpected links and attachments can be risky. If a message feels wrong, do not click. Use a website you type yourself. The LearnHere course Email & Online Safety Basics in Canada goes deeper on scams.

Keep a note of your device’s make and model in a safe place. If a phone is lost, Get Cyber Safe says to contact your service provider and report it to police — do not try to recover stolen property yourself.
