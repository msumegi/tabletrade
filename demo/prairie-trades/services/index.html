What the trucks carry

Work we will actually book.

Six kinds of call, named plainly. If yours is not on the list, phone anyway. We would rather say no than leave you guessing.

01 — Plumbing

Supply, drains, and the fixtures people touch.

Burst lines, slow drains, leaking valves, and new baths in houses that were plumbed when the yard was still a pasture. We carry repair couplings for the mix of copper, PEX, and the odd length of galvanized that shows up in Benchland basements.

If water is running where it should not, shut the house valve. If you are on a well and cannot find that valve, shut the pump breaker and call.

Quote this work

02 — Heating

Furnaces, boilers, and gas that has to be right.

No-heat calls jump the line from November through March. We service forced-air furnaces, boilers, and the propane setups common on acreages. New equipment is quoted with the venting and the gas line, not as a box on a pad.

Example gasfitter ticket and insurance live on the about page, marked as demo credentials.

Call the heat line

03 — Hot water

Tanks, tankless, and taps that shut off.

A tired tank usually announces itself with rust or a puddle. We will say if a repair is honest or if the heater is done. Tankless units need the gas supply and the vent sized properly — we check both before anyone falls in love with a brochure.

Kitchen and bath faucets, showers, and the small leaks that stain a ceiling are booked as ordinary work, not emergencies, unless water is going through a floor.

04 — Heat pumps & cooling

Cold country, with a few hot weeks.

Shoulder-season heat pumps and straight air conditioning for the July stretch. We size them for the house you have, including the addition that was never on the original drawing.

Heat pump

For houses that want one system to carry most of the year, with the furnace staying as the deep-cold backup.

Air conditioning

Added onto a furnace that is still worth keeping. We will not hang a condenser on a heat exchanger that is already failing.

05 — Acreage water

Pressure tanks, well pumps, and stock water.

Constant-pressure systems, pressure tanks that have gone waterlogged, and yard hydrants that split in April. We do not drill wells. We repair and replace the equipment between the well and the tap, and we will tell you when the problem is the well itself.

Bring the pump model if you know it. If you do not, a photo of the control box is enough to start.
